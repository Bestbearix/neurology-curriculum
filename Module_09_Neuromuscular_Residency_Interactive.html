Own the whole motor unit

The general-physician Module 9 teaches recognition and referral. This version assumes you localize along the motor unit, order and interpret EDX, prescribe modern immunotherapy and gene-targeted drugs, and manage the neuromuscular airway.

By the end you should be able to:
  1. Localize weakness to anterior horn, nerve, NMJ, or muscle — and predict the EDX.
  2. Diagnose ALS, exclude its treatable mimics (MMN, IBM, Kennedy), and use riluzole/tofersen.
  3. Manage GBS, CIDP and MMN by the EAN/PNS guidelines (and never give GBS steroids).
  4. Treat MG with pyridostigmine, immunotherapy, thymectomy, and the new biologics; recognise LEMS and botulism.
  5. Classify myopathies and channelopathies; monitor and act on neuromuscular respiratory failure.
Educational use only. Verify EDX interpretation, drugs, and monitoring against current guidelines and product labeling at the point of care.

Most likely level / diagnosis

Anterior horn cell: ALS & mimics

ALS = combined UMN + LMN degeneration, no sensory loss, contiguous spread, EMG active + chronic denervation across regions. Exclude mimics: cervical myelopathy, MMN with conduction block, inclusion-body myositis, Kennedy disease, thyroid/parathyroid disease.

Therapy. Riluzole (modest survival benefit) is standard. Tofersen — intrathecal antisense oligonucleotide lowering SOD1 — is the first gene-targeted ALS drug, only for SOD1-variant disease (VALOR/OLE: neurofilament reduction). Test genes early. Edaravone and pipeline agents (siRNA, PrimeC) evolving.
Multidisciplinary care drives survival: early NIV, nutrition/PEG, secretion/spasticity control, communication aids, goals of care; screen for frontotemporal dysfunction (~15%).
Treatable look-alikes: SMA (SMN-directed therapy now available) and Kennedy disease (X-linked androgen-receptor CAG expansion; perioral fasciculations, gynecomastia, sensory neuronopathy).

Peripheral nerve

Framework

Characterize by fibre type, distribution (length-dependent vs multifocal vs ganglionopathy), tempo, and axonal-vs-demyelinating on NCS.

GBS

IVIG (0.4 g/kg/day ×5) and plasma exchange are equally effective; steroids do NOT work. Monitor vital capacity and telemetry — ~20–30% need ventilation and autonomic instability can be lethal. Miller Fisher = ophthalmoplegia/ataxia/areflexia, anti-GQ1b.

CIDP & MMN

CIDP (EAN/PNS): first-line IVIG or corticosteroids; PLEX if refractory; IVIG first-line for motor CIDP. "CIDP variants" replaced "atypical CIDP". efgartigimod (ADHERE) newer; confirm diagnosis — CIDP is overdiagnosed.
MMN (EFNS/PNS): pure-motor, asymmetric, conduction block at non-compressible sites, ±anti-GM1, cold paresis. IVIG first-line; steroids/PLEX can WORSEN it. A key treatable ALS mimic.

Focal & plexus

Carpal tunnel (surgery > injection/splint for durable relief — DISTRICTS); ulnar (elbow); fibular (footdrop, spared inversion vs L5); neuralgic amyotrophy (Parsonage-Turner); diabetic lumbosacral radiculoplexus neuropathy (amyotrophy); radiation (painless, myokymia) vs neoplastic (painful) plexopathy.

Chemo-induced neuropathy: no agent firmly recommended for prevention; duloxetine has best evidence for painful CIPN (ASCO).

MG treatment (ABN 2025 + new biologics). Pyridostigmine; corticosteroids + steroid-sparing agents; thymectomy (thymoma; early in AChR+ generalised). Crisis: IVIG or PLEX. Targeted biologics: complement (eculizumab/ravulizumab/zilucoplan), FcRn (efgartigimod/rozanolixizumab), anti-IL-6 (satralizumab), anti-CD19 (inebilizumab), siRNA (cemdisiran), and BCMA CAR-T for refractory disease.
Avoid MG-worsening drugs (aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones, macrolides, magnesium). LEMS: treat the tumour + amifampridine. Botulism: give antitoxin early, don't await confirmation; support the airway.

Muscle disease

Inflammatory myopathies (5 subtypes)

  • Dermatomyositis — proximal weakness + heliotrope/Gottron; MDA5 → rapidly progressive ILD; malignancy screen.
  • Immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy — very high CK; anti-HMGCR (statin) / anti-SRP.
  • Antisynthetase syndrome — anti-Jo-1, ILD, mechanic's hands.
  • Overlap myositis; Inclusion-body myositis — older men, finger-flexor + quadriceps, treatment-refractory (ALS/CIDP mimic).

Dystrophies & genetic

Duchenne — corticosteroids + cardiorespiratory surveillance; exon-skipping, gene therapy, givinostat (EPIDYS). Becker, limb-girdle, FSHD, myotonic, oculopharyngodistal (GIPC1/RILPL1 repeat expansions), dystroglycanopathies.

Metabolic / toxic / channelopathy

Mitochondrial myopathy (ophthalmoplegia, exercise intolerance); statin myopathy (incl. persistent HMGCR necrotizing form). Channelopathies: non-dystrophic myotonias & periodic paralyses — SCN4A, CLCN1, CACNA1S, KCNJ2 (Andersen-Tawil); mexiletine for myotonia.

Neuromuscular emergencies & respiratory failure

The "20/30/40 rule": impending respiratory failure at FVC < 20 mL/kg, MIP less negative than −30 cm H₂O, or MEP < 40 cm H₂O. Rising RR, weak cough, staccato speech, orthopnea, paradoxical breathing, falling single-breath count = red flags. Intubate on the trajectory.
Oxygen saturation is a LATE sign — never rely on it in neuromuscular failure. NIV is for selected chronic/slow disease; avoid in rapidly worsening GBS/MG crisis with bulbar/secretion burden.

ICU-acquired weakness (critical-illness polyneuropathy/myopathy) and diaphragmatic dysfunction impair weaning. Envenomation (cobra/krait/Russell's viper) causes descending neuromuscular paralysis — support ventilation and give antivenom.

Flashcards — tap to flip

Case-based reasoning

Case 1. A 58-year-old has 6 months of progressive asymmetric hand weakness with cramps and fasciculations, brisk reflexes in a wasted limb, and no sensory symptoms. NCS shows motor conduction block in one forearm; anti-GM1 is positive.
Diagnosis and why it matters?

Pure-motor asymmetric weakness with conduction block and anti-GM1 = multifocal motor neuropathy, not ALS. This is treatable: give IVIG (first-line) and avoid steroids/PLEX, which can worsen it. Always look for conduction block before finalizing an ALS diagnosis.

Case 2. A 26-year-old develops ascending areflexic weakness over 5 days after diarrheal illness; CSF shows high protein with normal cells. FVC is falling on serial checks.
Treatment and the key monitoring?

Guillain-Barré syndrome. Treat with IVIG (0.4 g/kg/day ×5) or plasma exchange — equally effective; no steroids. Monitor vital capacity and telemetry; with a falling FVC toward the 20/30/40 thresholds, intubate electively and watch for autonomic instability. Don't wait for desaturation.

Case 3. A 45-year-old man with a SOD1 pathogenic variant has confirmed ALS. He asks whether any drug targets his genetics.
What do you offer?

Tofersen — an intrathecal antisense oligonucleotide lowering SOD1 — is approved specifically for SOD1-variant ALS and reduces neurofilament (VALOR/OLE). Add riluzole, consider edaravone, and wrap in multidisciplinary care (NIV, nutrition, goals of care). This case is why early genetic testing matters.
