Name the movement, then reason to a cause

▎What this module expects of you
  • Diagnose a movement disorder by watching it, not by imaging it. Phenomenology precedes aetiology in every case.
  • Know the MDS criteria for Parkinson disease and multiple system atrophy, and the consensus criteria for corticobasal degeneration, well enough to apply them at the bedside.
  • Be able to quote the landmark trial numbers and their eligibility criteria — LEAP, ADAGIO, EARLYSTIM, focused ultrasound, the VMAT2 trials.
  • Investigate in urgency tiers, because you are working where MRI, slit lamp and antibody panels are not available the same day.
  • Make functional movement disorder a positive diagnosis, and stop calling odd-looking dystonia functional.

The first split

Hypokinetic (parkinsonism) or hyperkinetic. Then name the hyperkinesia by speed, rhythm, suppressibility and pattern.

The tempo rule

Hyperacute or subacute onset means structural, metabolic, toxic, autoimmune or infectious — until proven otherwise. Years of progression means degenerative or genetic.

The under-50 rule

Any unexplained movement disorder under 50: actively exclude Wilson disease. Caeruloplasmin, 24-hour urinary copper, slit lamp.

The drug rule

Twelve months of drug history, asking about antiemetics and "dizziness" tablets by name. Drug-induced parkinsonism is the commonest reversible parkinsonism.

Re-emergent tremor

Arms outstretched, tremor vanishes, then returns after several seconds — that is Parkinson disease, not essential tremor. Count out loud so you wait long enough.

The null point

One head position where the tremor stops = dystonic head tremor. Essential tremor has no null point. This resolves most "refractory ET" referrals.

Five tools in this module

  • Phenomenology classifier — work from what you see to a named movement and a differential.
  • Tremor discriminator — separate ET, PD, dystonic and functional tremor on activation and bedside signs.
  • Investigation planner — ten scenarios mapped onto the five urgency tiers.
  • Red-flag sweep — a tri-state checklist for a parkinsonian patient, flagging what makes it atypical.
  • Drug-induced identifier — match timing and phenomenology to the syndrome and its treatment.
How to use this. Work the classifier and the planner on real patients from your own clinic list. The flashcards and quiz are for retention; the trial-criteria tab is for the viva, where you will be asked not what the trial showed but who it enrolled.

Core lessons

Twelve structured lessons. Each opens with what you must be able to do, then the detail.

1 · Phenomenology — the vocabulary
▎You must be able to
  • Name any hyperkinetic movement from observation alone.
  • State the one distinguishing question for each phenomenology.

Tremor is the only truly rhythmic hyperkinesia. Chorea flows and wanders, moving unpredictably from part to part, with motor impersistence — milkmaid grip, darting tongue. Ballism is chorea at the violent proximal end. Dystonia is patterned and directional, with overflow, mirror dystonia and a sensory trick. Myoclonus is shock-like and the fastest movement in neurology; negative myoclonus is asterixis. Tics have a premonitory urge and are temporarily suppressible. Stereotypies are identical each time and non-goal-directed, without an urge. Akathisia is a sensation first and a movement second.

The distinguishing questions. Tremor: is it rhythmic? Chorea: does it flow and wander? Dystonia: same pattern every time, and is there a sensory trick? Myoclonus: too fast to be anything else? Tic: is there an urge, and can they hold it in? Functional: does it change on distraction, and can you entrain it?

Two or more phenomenologies in one patient is common and is itself the clue — myoclonus-dystonia, dystonic tremor, parkinsonism with chorea.
2 · The diagnostic approach & tempo
▎You must be able to
  • Run the sequence: phenomenology → distribution → tempo → age → exposures → associated signs.
  • Use tempo to decide urgency.

Hyperacute (minutes–hours): stroke, hypo/hyperglycaemia, acute dystonic reaction, NMS, serotonin syndrome. Acute–subacute (days–weeks): autoimmune, infective and post-infectious, drug-induced, metabolic, vascular. Subacute–chronic (months): Wilson, NBIA, structural, tardive, paraneoplastic. Chronic (years): degenerative, genetic, essential tremor. Paroxysmal: paroxysmal dyskinesias, seizures, functional, hypoglycaemia.

Examine the eyes properly in every case — vertical supranuclear gaze palsy, square-wave jerks, slow saccades, apraxia of lid opening, oculogyric crisis, Kayser–Fleischer rings, opsoclonus. Atypical parkinsonism declares itself in the eyes first.

3 · Investigation strategy & urgency
▎You must be able to
  • Assign any investigation to a tier and justify it.
  • Say what to treat before the result arrives.

ANow: glucose, electrolytes with Ca and Mg, renal and liver function, ammonia if encephalopathic, TSH, FBC, CRP, ECG for QTc before any VMAT2 inhibitor, pregnancy test, drug chart.

BToday: CT head, blood gas with lactate, CK if rigid, cultures if febrile, LP, EEG if the myoclonus might be epileptic.

CThis week: caeruloplasmin and 24-hour urinary copper if under 50, B12, HIV and syphilis serology, ANA, ASO, peripheral smear for acanthocytes, ferritin and transferrin saturation.

DThis month, batched: MRI with SWI, slit lamp, autoimmune and paraneoplastic panels, genetics, DaTscan, malignancy screen.

EElective or omit: repeat imaging in stable chronic disease, DaTscan when the diagnosis is already secure, untargeted panels.

Make one referral trip do the work of three: MRI, slit lamp, panel and specialist review in a single attendance. Send the video and your results with the patient.
4 · Tremor
▎You must be able to
  • Apply the 3-year rule for essential tremor and the "ET plus" category.
  • Find a null point and a re-emergent tremor.

Essential tremor = isolated bilateral upper-limb action tremor for ≥3 years, nothing else on examination. ET plus adds soft signs — impaired tandem gait, questionable dystonic posturing, mild memory impairment. Definite dystonic, parkinsonian or cerebellar signs mean it is not ET at all.

Enhanced physiological tremor is reversible and common: thyrotoxicosis, β-agonists, theophylline, lithium, valproate, SSRIs, amiodarone, caffeine, alcohol withdrawal, hypoglycaemia, fever, anxiety.

Orthostatic tremor — 13–18 Hz, unsteadiness only on standing still, relieved by walking; palpate the thigh. Clonazepam, gabapentin, perampanel. Holmes tremor — slow, present at rest, posture and action, after a midbrain or thalamic lesion.

Focused ultrasound thalamotomy for ET: hand tremor improved 47% vs 0.1% sham, between-group difference 8.3 points. Paraesthesia and gait disturbance persisted at 12 months in 14% and 9%.
5 · Parkinson disease — diagnosis
▎You must be able to
  • Demonstrate decrement, not just slowness.
  • List the absolute exclusions and the red flags.

Core: bradykinesia plus rest tremor or rigidity. Bradykinesia means decrement — progressive reduction in amplitude or speed over 10–15 repetitions. Fatigue, spasticity and dysmetria slow movement without decrement.

Absolute exclusions: cerebellar signs; downward vertical supranuclear gaze palsy; bvFTD or PPA within 5 years; parkinsonism confined to the legs >3 years; dopamine-blocker exposure fitting drug-induced parkinsonism; no response to high-dose levodopa in at least moderate disease; cortical sensory loss, limb apraxia or progressive aphasia; normal presynaptic dopaminergic imaging.

Red flags: wheelchair within 5 years; no progression over 5 years; early bulbar or respiratory dysfunction; severe autonomic failure in 5 years; recurrent falls within 3 years; disproportionate anterocollis; no non-motor features after 5 years; unexplained pyramidal signs; bilateral symmetric throughout.

"No response to levodopa" only counts as an exclusion after an adequate trial — up to 1000 mg/day, several weeks, with objective assessment.
6 · Parkinson disease — therapy
▎You must be able to
  • Explain why delaying levodopa protects no one.
  • Ask about impulse-control disorders properly.
LEAP 5-year follow-up: starting levodopa 40 weeks earlier made no difference to total UPDRS (difference 0.24, P = 0.07), wearing-off prevalence or levodopa equivalent dose. Motor complications track disease duration and severity, not cumulative levodopa.
ADAGIO: rasagiline 1 mg met all three delayed-start endpoints; 2 mg met none. That dissociation is why neuroprotection is not established.
EARLYSTIM: STN DBS in patients 18–60 with ≥4 years disease and ≤3 years of motor complications improved PDQ-39 by 7.8 points while medical therapy worsened by 0.2 — difference 8.0 points, P = 0.002.

Amantadine is the drug for levodopa-induced dyskinesia. Anticholinergics only in tremor-predominant young patients, never over 65. Psychosis: reduce anticholinergics → amantadine → agonists → MAO-B → COMT, then quetiapine or clozapine only.

Impulse-control disorders are missed because nobody asks. Ask at every visit, ask the spouse, and ask about money specifically. Withdrawing the agonist can cause dopamine agonist withdrawal syndrome.
7 · Atypical parkinsonism
▎You must be able to
  • Name the earliest giveaway for PSP, MSA, CBS and DLB.
  • Say why DBS is not offered in atypical parkinsonism.

PSP — backward falls in year one, vertical supranuclear gaze palsy (down first), axial rigidity, applause sign; hummingbird sign. Variants include PSP-parkinsonism, which looks like PD for years. MSA — autonomic failure, cerebellar or parkinsonian, stridor, antecollis; hot-cross-bun sign. CBS — markedly asymmetric, apraxia, cortical sensory loss, myoclonus, alien limb. DLB — fluctuating cognition, visual hallucinations, RBD, neuroleptic sensitivity.

Nocturnal stridor in MSA is associated with sudden death. Ask the family about noisy breathing; involve ENT; consider CPAP.

Give everyone an adequate levodopa trial. Beyond that the work is symptomatic and multidisciplinary. DBS is not indicated — systematic review evidence shows poor, unsustained outcomes.

8 · Dystonia
▎You must be able to
  • Recognise the signatures: patterned, directional, sensory trick, overflow, mirror.
  • Give every young-onset dystonia a levodopa trial.

Cervical dystonia is commonest; botulinum toxin is first-line for all focal and segmental dystonia and is the most effective treatment in the whole field. Blepharospasm, oromandibular (with blepharospasm = Meige), laryngeal, writer's cramp. Generalized genetic dystonia responds well to GPi DBS.

Dopa-responsive dystonia (Segawa, GCH1): childhood gait dystonia with diurnal fluctuation, complete and permanent response to small doses of levodopa. The trial costs nothing; missing it costs a childhood.
Task specificity, a sensory trick and bizarre appearance are all features of organic dystonia. Do not call it functional for those reasons.
9 · Chorea & Huntington disease
▎You must be able to
  • Work up asymmetric chorea as structural or metabolic.
  • Run the safety checklist before a VMAT2 inhibitor.

Hemichorea → glucose and CT. Non-ketotic hyperglycaemia is a leading cause and usually resolves with glucose correction. The cheap tests that change the answer: glucose, peripheral smear for acanthocytes, CK, caeruloplasmin.

Huntington: CAG ≥40 fully penetrant, 36–39 reduced penetrance, 27–35 intermediate and expandable through the paternal line (anticipation). Tetrabenazine reduced chorea 5.0 vs 1.5 units; deutetrabenazine 12.1 → 7.7 vs 13.2 → 11.3.

Before either drug: QTc, depression screen, suicidality history, swallowing. Predictive testing follows a counselling protocol — never an add-on to a blood panel, never in an asymptomatic minor.
10 · Myoclonus, ataxia & RLS
▎You must be able to
  • Pick the drug from the generator.
  • Exclude the treatable ataxias and treat RLS iron properly.

Myoclonus: cortical → levetiracetam, valproate, piracetam. Subcortical/brainstem → clonazepam. Spinal segmental → treat the lesion. Unilateral asterixis is a focal lesion — hold the posture 30 seconds.

Ataxia: separate cerebellar from sensory from vestibular, then use tempo. Exclude thiamine, B12, vitamin E, hypothyroidism, coeliac, Wilson, anti-GAD, drugs. FGF14 / SCA27B is a common late-onset cause that panels missed and that often responds to 4-aminopyridine.

RLS: check ferritin and transferrin saturation in everyone — treat if ferritin <75 or TSAT <20%. Alpha-2-delta ligands are now preferred first-line over dopamine agonists because of augmentation.

Never treat augmentation with a bigger agonist dose. Symptoms spreading to the arms or starting earlier in the day means augmentation, not progression.
11 · Functional movement disorders
▎You must be able to
  • Elicit and demonstrate entrainment, distractibility and Hoover.
  • Deliver the explanation, which is itself the treatment.

A positive rule-in diagnosis. Distractibility, entrainment, variability, incongruence, suggestibility, co-activation sign, Hoover sign, whack-a-mole sign. Functional and organic disorders coexist in a substantial minority.

Explaining it: name it; show them the sign; explain software not hardware; say it is not imagined and not their fault and is potentially reversible; refer to physiotherapy with a functional-disorder approach.

Two errors in opposite directions — calling odd organic dystonia functional, and missing a functional overlay in established PD, which leads to endless futile dopaminergic escalation.
12 · Drug-induced disorders & emergencies
▎You must be able to
  • Separate NMS from serotonin syndrome on reflexes and pupils.
  • Prevent parkinsonism-hyperpyrexia in your own ward.

Timing separates the drug-induced syndromes: acute dystonic reaction hours–days; akathisia days–weeks; parkinsonism weeks–months; tardive syndromes months–years.

NMS — lead-pipe rigidity, hyporeflexia, days to weeks after a dopamine blocker. Serotonin syndrome — within 24 hours, clonus and hyperreflexia greatest in the legs, mydriasis, diarrhoea; cyproheptadine.

The commonest way a hospital causes parkinsonism-hyperpyrexia is by making a PD patient nil by mouth. Prescribe exact levodopa timings; if they cannot swallow, use a rotigotine patch or NG tube. Never simply omit doses.

Anticholinergics treat acute dystonic reaction and tardive dystonia but worsen tardive dyskinesia. VMAT2 inhibitors are the evidence-based treatment for TD.

Drug-induced movement disorder identifier

▎Timing separates them
  • Acute dystonic reaction — hours to days. Akathisia — days to weeks. Parkinsonism — weeks to months. Tardive — months to years.
  • Anticholinergics treat acute dystonic reaction and tardive dystonia, but worsen tardive dyskinesia.
