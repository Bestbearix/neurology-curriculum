Movement Disorders

Residency deep-dive · PGY-3 subspecialty · classify by phenomenology, then treat on the evidence
Resident level Phenomenology Parkinson trials VMAT2 / botox Focused ultrasound

Name the movement, then reason to a cause

The general-physician Module 6 teaches recognition of common presentations. This version assumes you fix the phenomenology, separate hypo- from hyperkinetic disorders, diagnose PD and its mimics, and prescribe on the trial evidence.

By the end you should be able to:
  1. Classify a movement disorder by phenomenology (tremor, chorea, dystonia, tics, myoclonus, parkinsonism).
  2. Diagnose Parkinson disease and flag "parkinson-plus" red flags.
  3. Build PD therapy and cite LEAP, ADAGIO, and EARLYSTIM; screen for impulse-control disorders.
  4. Treat tremor (incl. focused-ultrasound thalamotomy), dystonia (botulinum toxin), chorea/TD (VMAT2 inhibitors), and tics (CBIT).
  5. Rule in functional movement disorders on positive signs.
Educational use only. Verify drugs, doses, and device indications against current guidelines and product labeling at the point of care.

Most likely category

Parkinson disease

Parkinsonism = bradykinesia + rest tremor and/or rigidity. Idiopathic PD: asymmetric onset, 4–6 Hz pill-rolling rest tremor, robust levodopa response; non-motor prodrome (hyposmia, REM sleep behavior disorder, constipation). Pathology = α-synuclein Lewy disease with nigrostriatal loss (LRRK2/GBA/PRKN and polygenic contributions).

Parkinson-plus red flags: early falls, early autonomic failure, symmetric onset, poor levodopa response, early dementia/hallucinations, vertical gaze palsy, cerebellar signs.

Therapy & landmark trials

LEAP (early vs delayed levodopa). No disease-modifying effect — levodopa is symptomatic, does not accelerate progression, and there's no benefit to withholding it when symptoms warrant.
ADAGIO (delayed-start rasagiline). Dose-inconsistent (1 mg met, 2 mg failed the hierarchical endpoints) → disease modification not established; MAO-B inhibitors are symptomatic/adjunctive.
EARLYSTIM. Subthalamic DBS added at the stage of early motor complications improved quality of life and motor disability vs best medical therapy.
Dopamine-agonist impulse-control disorders (gambling, hypersexuality, shopping/eating) — screen every visit; reduce/rotate the agonist if they appear.

Hyperkinetic disorders & therapy

Tremor / essential tremor

First-line: propranolol, primidone. Refractory: MRI-guided focused-ultrasound thalamotomy (VIM) or DBS.

Focused ultrasound (RCT + 3-yr follow-up). Unilateral incisionless VIM thalamotomy durably reduces medication-refractory hand tremor vs sham; gait/sensory effects are the main risks — treat one side.

Dystonia

Botulinum toxin first-line for focal dystonia (daxi-/abobotulinumtoxinA evidence provided); oral agents and GPi DBS for generalized/refractory. Always trial levodopa in young-onset dystonia (dopa-responsive dystonia).

Chorea / Huntington / tardive dyskinesia

VMAT2 inhibitors. Tetrabenazine/deutetrabenazine reduce Huntington chorea (deutetrabenazine better tolerated); valbenazine & deutetrabenazine are the approved treatments for tardive dyskinesia.
Tetrabenazine: depression/suicidality and parkinsonism are dose-limiting — screen mood. Consider hyperglycemic chorea (striatal T1 hyperintensity) in acute unilateral chorea.

Tics / Tourette & RLS

CBIT / habit-reversal therapy gives sustained tic reduction and is first-line; add alpha-2 agonists (clonidine/guanfacine), VMAT2 inhibitors, or antipsychotics for impairing tics.

RLS: correct iron deficiency (ferritin), then alpha-2-delta ligands (gabapentin/pregabalin) first-line; dopamine agonists second-line due to augmentation.

Functional movement disorders: diagnose positively on inconsistency — distractibility, variability, and tremor entrainment — not by exclusion. Deliver the diagnosis clearly and refer to FND-informed physiotherapy/psychology.

Case-based reasoning

Case 1. A 68-year-old has an asymmetric 5 Hz rest tremor, bradykinesia, and cogwheel rigidity, with a strong response to levodopa. Six months later he develops new compulsive gambling.
Diagnosis + what caused the new behavior?

Idiopathic Parkinson disease. The gambling is a dopamine-agonist impulse-control disorder — reduce or rotate off the agonist and counsel the family. LEAP tells us levodopa itself isn't disease-modifying but is appropriate for symptom control.

Case 2. A 72-year-old has a bilateral action tremor of the hands and voice, relieved by wine, unresponsive to propranolol and primidone, severely limiting writing and eating.
Next options?

Medication-refractory essential tremor. Offer MRI-guided focused-ultrasound thalamotomy (VIM) — a randomized sham-controlled trial and 3-year follow-up show durable benefit — or VIM DBS. Treat one side to limit gait/sensory adverse effects.

Case 3. A 45-year-old with a family history of a neurodegenerative illness develops brief, random, flowing movements, irritability, and declining cognition.
Diagnosis + symptomatic chorea treatment?

Huntington disease (autosomal-dominant CAG expansion in HTT). For disabling chorea use a VMAT2 inhibitor — deutetrabenazine (better tolerated) or tetrabenazine — while screening for depression/suicidality. Provide genetic counseling and psychiatric support.
