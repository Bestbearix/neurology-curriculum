Why this module?

Most neurology in adults is the neurological face of systemic disease. AF causes embolic stroke; SLE causes neuropsychiatric syndromes; sickle cell causes childhood stroke; APS causes recurrent thrombosis; sarcoidosis causes basal meningitis; HIV causes opportunistic infections and cognitive decline; transplant immunosuppression causes PRES and PML; radiation causes necrosis and leukoencephalopathy. The general physician's job is to recognise systemic clues in any new neurological syndrome and to coordinate multidisciplinary care.

After this module you will:

  • Match common cardiac, renal, endocrine, haematological, autoimmune, and infectious systemic conditions to their characteristic neurological manifestations.
  • Differentiate SLE neuropsychiatric syndromes, APS, vasculitides, and lupus-mimics at the bedside.
  • Apply guideline-aligned workups: transcranial Doppler in sickle cell; APS antibody triple; ADAMTS13 for TTP; ACE / chest CT / PET for sarcoid; HLA-B*57 / HIV testing.
  • Choose appropriate anticoagulation in AF, APS, and IE; recognise PFO closure indications.
  • Recognise transplant-related neurology (calcineurin inhibitor toxicity, PRES, opportunistic infections, PTLD).
  • Differentiate radiation necrosis from tumour recurrence and treat both.
  • Avoid the systemic-disease traps that drive iatrogenic harm — uncritical DOAC in APS, missed thiamine after bariatric surgery, missed Wernicke in hyperemesis, missed B12 in IBD or post-bariatric.
The single principle: in any new neurological syndrome, do a systems review and basic systemic workup. Many neurological emergencies (TTP, GCA, eclampsia, lupus cerebritis, antiphospholipid catastrophic syndrome, sickle cell stroke, IE cerebral embolus, hepatic encephalopathy) declare themselves only when the systemic clues are sought.

Lessons

1. Cardiac disease and the brain
  • Atrial fibrillation: ~5× stroke risk; CHA₂DS₂-VASc; DOAC preferred over warfarin except mechanical valves + rheumatic mitral stenosis (warfarin). Cross-ref Module 2.
  • Patent foramen ovale: cryptogenic stroke in young + high-risk PFO features; CLOSE/REDUCE/RESPECT-extended → closure reduces recurrence in selected < 60 y.
  • Infective endocarditis: up to 35% have neurological complications — septic embolic stroke, mycotic aneurysm (distal MCA branches), brain abscess, meningitis. Anticoagulation controversial in IE — usually held. Cardiac surgery timing: early reasonable for ischaemic without haemorrhagic conversion; delay 2–4 weeks for haemorrhagic.
  • Cardiac surgery: peri-op stroke ~2% CABG; post-pump cognitive decline; perioperative AF.
  • Takotsubo: catecholamine-mediated apical ballooning; severe SAH/ICH classic precipitants; supportive care, reverses 1–4 weeks.
  • Cardiac arrest: HIE + multimodal neuroprognostication ≥ 72 h (Module 22; TTM2).
  • Heart failure: cognitive impairment; treat SDB (CPAP if OSA; ASV contraindicated in HFrEF EF ≤ 45 per SERVE-HF — Module 14).
2. Renal disease, dialysis, electrolytes
  • Uraemic encephalopathy (Module 13): GFR < 10; asterixis + multifocal myoclonus + EEG triphasic; reverses with dialysis.
  • Dialysis disequilibrium: cerebral oedema from rapid urea clearance; short gentle initial sessions.
  • Dialysis dementia (now rare; aluminium).
  • Transplant neurology: calcineurin inhibitor toxicity (tremor, PRES, seizures, neuropathy); opportunistic infections (PML, Listeria, CMV, toxo); PTLD with CNS.
  • Electrolytes (Module 13): hyponatraemia + CPM if corrected > 8 mmol/L/24 h; hypercalcaemia + hypocalcaemia + hypomagnesaemia + hypoglycaemia / hyperglycaemia; hyperammonaemia.

4. Haematological disease
  • Sickle cell: ~10% of children stroke by 20 untreated; STOP TCD screening from age 2; TCD > 200 cm/s → chronic transfusion to keep HbS < 30% (or hydroxyurea per TWiTCH 2016); silent infarcts cause cognitive impairment.
  • Polycythaemia vera: JAK2 V617F; venesection HCT < 45%.
  • Leukaemia: leptomeningeal infiltration, intrathecal MTX in ALL; hyperviscosity (AML blast crisis); chemo neurotoxicity (vincristine, MTX leukoencephalopathy, ifosfamide encephalopathy).
  • TTP: pentad (40% only) — MAHA + thrombocytopenia + fever + renal + neuro; ADAMTS13; PLEX daily + caplacizumab HERCULES + steroid + rituximab; do NOT wait for ADAMTS13 result.
  • APS: arterial + venous thrombosis + recurrent fetal loss; lupus anticoagulant + anti-cardiolipin + anti-β2-GP-I; CAPS = emergency anticoag + steroid + PLEX/IVIG; warfarin INR 2–3 — DOAC LESS EFFECTIVE in APS per TRAPS, AVOID in triple-positive.
  • Paraneoplastic + POEMS + Waldenström hyperviscosity + myeloma cord compression (Module 10) + MGUS neuropathy.
5. Rheumatological / autoimmune
  • SLE NPSLE (ACR 19 syndromes): cognitive + mood + psychosis + seizures + stroke (often APS) + transverse myelitis + headache + chorea; ANA + dsDNA + low C3/C4; treat with steroid + cyclophosphamide / MMF / rituximab.
  • Sjögren: SF / autonomic neuropathy + sensory ganglionopathy + cranial neuropathies + transverse myelitis / NMO-like; anti-Ro/La; treat immunosuppression; rituximab.
  • RA: cervical myelopathy from atlantoaxial subluxation — pre-op flexion/extension films or MRI; CTS + ulnar at elbow; rheumatoid vasculitis + mononeuritis multiplex.
  • Vasculitides table: GCA (visual loss → empirical IV methylpred + biopsy + tocilizumab — Module 19); GPA pachymeningitis + cranial neuropathies cANCA/PR3 + rituximab/CYC; EGPA mononeuritis multiplex pANCA/MPO + asthma + eosinophilia + mepolizumab; PAN mononeuritis multiplex medium-vessel + HBV historical + CYC; PACNS multifocal stroke + encephalopathy + CSF inflammation + DSA beaded narrowing + brain biopsy gold standard + CYC; RCVS recurrent thunderclap + segmental vasoconstriction REVERSING in 1-3 months (vs PACNS chronic); Takayasu large-vessel young women.
  • Neurosarcoidosis (~5% of sarcoidosis): cranial neuropathies (facial, optic), chronic basal meningitis, hypothalamic / pituitary (DI), myelopathy; MRI leptomeningeal basal; CSF lymphocytic + low glucose mimicking TB; ACE + chest CT + PET-CT + biopsy; corticosteroid + MTX/MMF/infliximab.
  • Behçet: oral + genital ulcers + uveitis + brainstem syndrome / CVST; treat steroid + biologics.
  • IgG4-related: hypertrophic pachymeningitis + orbital pseudotumour + hypophysitis; IgG4 + biopsy + steroid + rituximab.
6. GI, respiratory, HIV, transplantation, radiation
  • Coeliac: gluten ataxia + neuropathy + epilepsy with bilateral occipital calcifications + B12 deficiency; coeliac serology in unexplained ataxia / neuropathy.
  • IBD: stroke (hypercoagulable) + CVST + B12 after ileal resection.
  • Bariatric surgery: Wernicke from thiamine deficiency (Module 13, 20); B12 + copper deficiencies.
  • Whipple disease: oculomasticatory myorhythmia is pathognomonic; ceftriaxone + TMP-SMX.
  • Respiratory: SDB Module 14; CO₂ narcosis Module 13; NM respiratory failure Module 9.
  • HIV: HAND nosology; OIs Module 11 (toxo + PCNSL + crypto AMBITION-cm + PML + CMV + TB); IRIS; neurosyphilis (tabes + general paresis + meningovascular + Argyll-Robertson).
  • Transplant: calcineurin neurotoxicity (PRES, tremor, seizures); OIs; PTLD; CPM/ODS; critical illness PN.
  • Radiation: acute oedema → steroids; early-delayed somnolence; late-delayed necrosis (mimics recurrence — MRS/PET differentiate; bevacizumab); leukoencephalopathy; cavernous malformations; secondary tumours; pituitary; spinal myelopathy; radiation plexopathy painless + myokymia (vs malignant infiltration painful).

Case Vignettes

Case 1 — A 35-year-old woman with prior DVT, miscarriage, and livedo reticularis presents with sudden left hemiparesis. MRI: right MCA infarct. Lupus anticoagulant + anti-cardiolipin + anti-β2-glycoprotein-I all positive (triple positive)

No prior anticoagulation.

Diagnosis: Antiphospholipid syndrome (triple-positive) with arterial thrombotic stroke.

Plan:

  1. Acute stroke care per Module 2 (thrombolysis if within window and no contraindication; thrombectomy if LVO).
  2. Long-term anticoagulation with warfarin INR 2.0–3.0 (some recommend higher INR 3.0–4.0 for recurrent arterial APS).
  3. Avoid DOACs in triple-positive APS — TRAPS trial (Pengo Blood 2018) showed rivaroxaban INFERIOR to warfarin; EULAR 2019 advises against DOAC in triple-positive APS.
  4. Aspirin if pregnant or planning pregnancy (Module 20).
  5. Workup for SLE (~30% of APS have SLE) — ANA, dsDNA, C3/C4.
  6. Counsel about pregnancy planning (aspirin + LMWH from positive pregnancy test).
  7. Lifelong anticoagulation; rheumatology / haematology referral.
Case 2 — A 38-year-old woman has subacute confusion, fever, fatigue, and now generalized seizures. Hb 8.5 g/dL, platelets 18 × 10⁹/L, creatinine 230, schistocytes on blood film, normal coagulation. ADAMTS13 activity result pending

LDH markedly raised; haptoglobin undetectable.

Diagnosis: Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP).

Plan:

  1. Plasma exchange daily — START IMMEDIATELY, do NOT wait for ADAMTS13 result.
  2. Caplacizumab (anti-vWF nanobody) per HERCULES trial (Scully NEJM 2019) — reduces time to platelet normalisation and recurrence; usually combined with PLEX + steroid.
  3. Corticosteroid: methylprednisolone 1 g/day × 3 days then prednisolone taper.
  4. Rituximab if refractory or relapsing.
  5. AVOID PLATELET TRANSFUSION unless life-threatening bleeding — risk of worsening microvascular thrombosis.
  6. Treat seizures (LEV).
  7. Investigate trigger: pregnancy (Module 20), infection, drugs (ticlopidine, ciclosporin), connective tissue disease, malignancy.
  8. Untreated TTP mortality > 90%; treated < 20%.
Case 3 — A 14-year-old boy with HbSS sickle cell disease has a TCD velocity of 220 cm/s on annual screening

He has never had a clinical stroke. MRI brain shows silent infarcts in deep white matter.

Diagnosis: Sickle cell with abnormal TCD (≥ 200 cm/s) — high stroke risk.

Plan:

  1. Chronic transfusion programme to maintain HbS < 30% (STOP trial Adams NEJM 1998 — substantially reduces first stroke).
  2. TWiTCH trial (Ware Lancet 2016): after at least one year of stable chronic transfusion, hydroxyurea (with regular monitoring) is NON-INFERIOR to chronic transfusion for maintaining TCD velocities — many programmes now transition to hydroxyurea after initial stabilisation.
  3. Iron overload management (chelation with deferasirox or deferoxamine).
  4. Cognitive surveillance — silent infarcts cause neurocognitive impairment even without clinical stroke.
  5. Consider haematopoietic stem cell transplant for severe disease or when transfusion not feasible.
  6. Standard sickle cell care: penicillin prophylaxis, vaccinations, hydration, pain plan, transition to adult care.
Case 4 — A 28-year-old woman with known SLE presents with new psychosis, headache, and a focal seizure. MRI: multiple bilateral T2 hyperintensities. CSF: lymphocytic pleocytosis with elevated protein. ANA +, dsDNA elevated, C3 + C4 low. Antiphospholipid antibody negative

Active SLE flare with multi-organ involvement (rash + arthritis + low complement).

Diagnosis: Neuropsychiatric SLE (NPSLE) flare — likely lupus cerebritis.

Plan:

  1. Stop any drugs that could mimic (steroids → psychosis, NSAIDs).
  2. High-dose IV methylprednisolone 1 g/day × 3–5 days followed by oral prednisolone 1 mg/kg with taper.
  3. IV cyclophosphamide (Euro-Lupus or NIH regimen) or rituximab for severe CNS lupus.
  4. Mycophenolate as alternative or maintenance.
  5. Treat seizures with LEV (no enzyme interactions).
  6. Rule out CNS infection (CSF for bacteria, viruses, fungi); exclude PRES from steroid / hypertension; exclude APS thrombotic event.
  7. Workup: complete APS panel; full SLE workup; ophthalmology for eye involvement.
  8. Long-term: hydroxychloroquine; steroid-sparing immunosuppression; address contraception (avoid oestrogen if APS).
Case 5 — A 45-year-old man with sarcoidosis presents with progressive bilateral facial weakness, diabetes insipidus, and bitemporal hemianopia. MRI: leptomeningeal enhancement particularly at the base of the brain and around the optic chiasm. CSF: lymphocytic pleocytosis with low glucose

ACE elevated. Chest CT: bilateral hilar adenopathy. Mediastinal lymph node biopsy: non-caseating granulomata.

Diagnosis: Neurosarcoidosis with chronic basal meningitis, bilateral facial neuropathies, hypothalamic involvement (DI), and chiasmal compression.

Plan:

  1. High-dose corticosteroid: IV methylprednisolone 1 g/day × 3–5 days → oral prednisolone 1 mg/kg with slow taper.
  2. Steroid-sparing immunosuppression: methotrexate or mycophenolate as first-line; infliximab (TNF-α inhibitor) for refractory disease — increasingly used as second-line.
  3. Endocrine replacement: desmopressin for DI (Module 12, 19).
  4. Ophthalmology assessment for chiasmal involvement and uveitis.
  5. Rule out TB (sarcoid CSF mimics TB — Module 11); exclude HIV.
  6. Address comorbidities: cardiac sarcoid (ECG, echo, cardiac MRI), pulmonary sarcoid (PFTs), hepatic involvement.
  7. Multidisciplinary care: neurology + ophthalmology + ID + cardiology + endocrinology + pulmonology + rheumatology.
  8. Long-term cognitive surveillance; monitor for relapse on steroid taper.
Case 6 — A 60-year-old kidney transplant recipient on tacrolimus develops headache + cortical visual disturbance + seizures. BP 195/115. MRI: bilateral posterior parieto-occipital T2/FLAIR hyperintensity

Tacrolimus trough elevated above target.

Diagnosis: Posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome (PRES) — calcineurin inhibitor and hypertension triggered (Module 13).

Plan:

  1. Control BP gently: target 25% reduction in 1–2 h, then 160/110 over 24 h (labetalol or nicardipine infusion); avoid abrupt large drops (cerebral hypoperfusion).
  2. Reduce or rotate calcineurin inhibitor — consider switching to belatacept or mTOR inhibitor (sirolimus, everolimus) in consultation with transplant team.
  3. Treat seizures (LEV preferred — no interactions; AVOID enzyme inducers like CBZ / phenytoin which affect tacrolimus metabolism).
  4. Correct electrolytes (Mg, K).
  5. Avoid aggravators (calcineurin inhibitor + bevacizumab + cytotoxic chemo).
  6. MRI follow-up in 1–2 weeks to confirm reversal.
  7. Most reverse within days–weeks. Counsel about future PRES risk; monitor tacrolimus levels closely.
  8. Cross-ref Modules 13, 20.
