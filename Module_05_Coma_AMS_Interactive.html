Why this module?

The unresponsive patient is one of the most stressful presentations in general medicine. Many causes are reversible, but the diagnostic time pressure is high — brainstem catastrophe, herniation, non-convulsive status epilepticus, hypoglycaemia, and meningoencephalitis all have minute-counting consequences. This module gives you a fast structured approach.

After this module you will:

  • Run the first 5 min of a coma resuscitation including the modern "coma cocktail".
  • Perform Plum & Posner's four-pillar exam: motor, respiration, pupils, eye movements.
  • Sort coma into supratentorial / infratentorial / metabolic.
  • Recognise the herniation syndromes and Cushing's response.
  • Use GCS and the FOUR score correctly (and know when each is preferred).
  • Diagnose and manage delirium with the CAM, RASS, and ABCDEF bundle.
  • Know prerequisites and the clinical checklist for brain-death declaration.
The one principle to anchor everything: in coma, pupillary reactivity is preserved in metabolic coma and lost (asymmetrically or symmetrically) in structural coma. Make the pupils your first localising sign.

Lessons

One panel per sitting.

1. Defining the disorders of consciousness

Consciousness has two components: arousal (wakefulness, ARAS) and awareness (cortex + thalamus). Disorders affect one, the other, or both.

  • Coma — no arousal, no awareness. Eyes closed.
  • Vegetative / UWS — arousal preserved (eyes open), no awareness.
  • Minimally conscious — reproducible low-level purposeful behaviour.
  • Locked-in — full awareness, only vertical eye movement and blinking preserved.
  • Delirium — fluctuating disordered cognition with inattention.
  • Brain death — irreversible loss of all brain & brainstem function + apnoea.
Always ask: "blink twice if you hear me," "look up." This may be the only voluntary response a locked-in patient has.
2. The first 5 minutes — ABC + coma cocktail
  1. Airway / breathing / circulation. Intubate if GCS ≤ 8 or airway at risk.
  2. BP, HR, temperature. Cushing's response (HTN + bradycardia + irregular respiration) → impending herniation.
  3. Stat glucose finger-stick. Hypoglycaemia → thiamine 100 mg IV first, then D50W 50 mL IV.
  4. Naloxone 0.4 mg IV if opioid overdose possible.
  5. Empirical antibiotics + aciclovir if meningoencephalitis suspected.
  6. Cervical-spine precautions if trauma not excluded.
Always thiamine before glucose in malnourished or alcohol-use patients — glucose alone precipitates Wernicke encephalopathy.
3. The four-pillar coma exam (Plum & Posner)
  1. Motor responses: follows commands > localises > withdraws > decorticate flexion > decerebrate extension > none.
  2. Respiratory pattern: Cheyne–Stokes (cortical/metabolic) · hyperventilation (acidosis, brainstem) · apneustic (pons) · ataxic/Biot (medulla).
  3. Pupils: pinpoint reactive (pons/opioid) · mid-position fixed (midbrain) · unilateral blown (uncal/CN III) · bilateral fixed dilated (severe brainstem, anoxia, anticholinergic, severe barbiturate, hypothermia).
  4. Eye movements: roving (intact brainstem) · gaze deviation toward destructive lesion · ocular bobbing (pons) · skew (brainstem/cerebellum) · doll's eyes / cold caloric to test brainstem.
Caloric in the awake patient: COWS (Cold-Opposite, Warm-Same) describes the fast-phase nystagmus. In coma there's no fast phase — you see only tonic deviation toward the cold ear if brainstem is intact.

6. GCS and FOUR score

GCS 3–15. Eye (4) + Verbal (5) + Motor (6). Always document each component. GCS ≤ 8 → intubate.

FOUR score 0–16. Eye + Motor + Brainstem reflexes + Respiration. Works for intubated patients; includes brainstem function. Detects locked-in syndrome (high E score).

7. Differential by category

Categories: vascular, trauma, infection, metabolic, toxic, seizure, mass, endocrine, autoimmune, psychiatric mimic.

Practical clues from the bedside: fever (sepsis, infection, heatstroke) · hypothermia (sepsis, exposure, hypothyroid, hypoglycaemia) · cherry-red skin (CO) · jaundice/hepatic fetor (hepatic) · acetone breath (DKA) · uraemic frost · Battle sign (basilar skull fracture) · papilloedema (raised ICP) · neck stiffness only on flexion (meningitis, SAH, foramen magnum herniation) · incontinence (unwitnessed seizure).

Two missed diagnoses to remember: non-convulsive status epilepticus (subtle twitching, unexplained obtundation — get cEEG) and HSV encephalitis (fever + altered mentation + temporal-lobe MRI changes — treat with aciclovir).
8. Delirium

Most common AMS the general physician sees. 50% post-stroke, 80% in ICU. Hypoactive form is more common and more often missed.

CAM criteria (need 1 + 2 + (3 OR 4)):

  1. Acute onset & fluctuating course
  2. Inattention
  3. Disorganised thinking
  4. Altered level of consciousness

Causes — I WATCH DEATH: Infection · Withdrawal · Acute metabolic · Trauma · CNS pathology · Hypoxia · Deficiencies (thiamine, B12) · Endocrine · Acute vascular · Toxins · Heavy metals.

Management: Treat cause. Non-pharm first (orient, glasses/hearing, mobilise, sleep hygiene). Avoid restraints, BZDs, anticholinergics. Low-dose haloperidol only for severe agitation. ABCDEF bundle in ICU.

9. Brain death determination

Brain death = irreversible loss of all brain & brainstem function + apnoea, with all confounders excluded. Clinical diagnosis; ancillary tests only when apnoea cannot be done.

  1. Prerequisites: known catastrophic cause + imaging consistent + no sedatives (wait 5–7 half-lives) + no NMB + normal electrolytes/endocrine + temp ≥ 36 °C + SBP ≥ 100 + no spontaneous breaths.
  2. Clinical exam (all absent): pupils (mid-position, non-reactive), corneal, oculocephalic, oculovestibular (50 mL ice water × 2 min each), facial movement to supraorbital pressure, gag, cough, motor to noxious in all 4 limbs.
  3. Apnoea test: pre-oxygenate, normocapnia baseline, disconnect ventilator with O₂ insufflation 6 L/min, observe 8–10 min. Positive: PaCO₂ ≥ 60 mmHg or rise ≥ 20 mmHg with no breaths.
  4. Ancillary if apnoea not done: EEG (≥ 30 min electrocerebral silence), CTA / 4-vessel angiography (no flow above proximal ICA), nuclear flow study.

Spinal reflexes (e.g. triple flexion) can persist after brain death and don't invalidate the diagnosis. Notify the organ procurement organisation before withdrawal discussion.

10. Red flags & when to refer
  • Coma of unknown cause — admit ICU/HDU.
  • Unilateral fixed dilated pupil — uncal herniation, neurosurgery NOW.
  • Cushing's triad (HTN + bradycardia + irregular respiration) — herniation imminent.
  • Cerebellar haemorrhage or infarct of any size — neurosurgery NOW (delayed swelling kills).
  • Subtle twitching + unexplained obtundation — suspect non-convulsive SE, get cEEG.
  • Fever + altered mentation — start empirical antimicrobials + aciclovir, then LP.
  • Young patient with rapid neuropsychiatric decline — consider autoimmune encephalitis.

Case Vignettes

Case 1 — A 28-year-old found unresponsive at home

BP 110/70, HR 60, RR 6, SpO₂ 88% on room air. Pupils 2 mm bilaterally, sluggish reactivity (visible only with magnifier). GCS E1 V1 M2. Track marks in antecubital fossae.

Diagnosis: opioid overdose.

Plan: Bag-mask ventilate; naloxone 0.4 mg IV (repeat as needed). Watch for re-narcotisation. Check glucose. Coma cocktail (thiamine + glucose if low). Investigate co-ingestants. Withdrawal precautions.

Case 2 — A 65-year-old with sudden left hemiparesis, then decreased consciousness

Hour 2 after onset: GCS E2 V2 M4. Right pupil 6 mm, unreactive; left pupil 3 mm, reactive. Right gaze deviation. Decorticate posturing on the left side. BP 200/110, HR 50, irregular respiration.

Localization: right hemispheric mass effect (large MCA stroke or haemorrhage) with uncal herniation (right blown pupil). Cushing's triad present → imminent further herniation.

Plan: Intubate (GCS ≤ 8 + airway). Head-up 30°. Hyperventilate to PaCO₂ ~30 as a bridge. Mannitol 1–1.5 g/kg or 23.4% hypertonic saline 30 mL via central line. Stat non-contrast CT. Neurosurgery and neuro-ICU now. If haemorrhagic: BP 130–150. If ischaemic with malignant MCA: hemicraniectomy candidate.

Case 3 — A 22-year-old with 5 days of fever, headache, then coma

Temp 39.4 °C. Neck stiff. GCS E2 V2 M4. Generalised tonic-clonic seizure witnessed in ED. CT normal. MRI shows bilateral temporal-lobe hyperintensity.

Diagnosis: HSV encephalitis until proven otherwise.

Plan: Start IV aciclovir 10 mg/kg q8h immediately — do not wait for LP. Empirical antibiotics (ceftriaxone + vancomycin) and dexamethasone in case bacterial. LP after CT (CSF: lymphocytic, RBCs common in HSV; send HSV PCR). EEG to exclude status. Antiseizure therapy (levetiracetam or fosphenytoin loading). ICU admission.

Case 4 — A 75-year-old four days post-hip surgery with fluctuating confusion

Episodes of agitation alternating with drowsiness. Inattentive — cannot count backwards from 20. Disorganised speech. No fever. Multiple medications including opioids and a long-acting benzodiazepine for "sleep". Daughter says he is normally sharp and lives independently.

Diagnosis: postoperative delirium (mixed type). CAM positive.

Plan: Treat the cause(s) — stop the BZD, minimise opioid load with multimodal analgesia, screen for infection (urine, chest), check electrolytes/glucose, check for constipation and urinary retention. Restore glasses/hearing aids, family at bedside, daylight, mobilise. Avoid restraints. Low-dose haloperidol only if severely agitated and at risk of harm. Document and reassess daily; if no improvement, image and consider EEG (rule out NCSE).

Case 5 — A 50-year-old comatose after cardiac arrest with absent brainstem reflexes

Day 4 post-arrest. Off sedation > 72 h. CT: diffuse cerebral oedema, loss of grey-white differentiation, obliterated basal cisterns. Pupils mid-position, fixed. No corneal, oculocephalic, oculovestibular, gag, or cough. No motor to noxious in 4 limbs (a triple-flexion spinal reflex on lower limb pinch). Temp 36.4 °C, BP 110/65 on low-dose noradrenaline, SpO₂ 99% on FiO₂ 0.4.

Next step: brain-death determination per checklist. Confirm no residual sedatives (5–7 half-lives), no NMB (train-of-four). All clinical findings absent → proceed to apnoea test: pre-oxygenate to PaO₂ ≥ 200, normocapnia baseline, disconnect with O₂ insufflation 6 L/min, observe 8–10 min, repeat ABG. PaCO₂ ≥ 60 or rise ≥ 20 with no breaths = positive. Document time of second ABG as time of death. Notify organ procurement organisation.
