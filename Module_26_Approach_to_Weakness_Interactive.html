Why this module?

Weakness is the symptom that triggers more neurology referrals than any other. A structured approach — tempo + pattern + level — turns a vague complaint into a localisable, treatable problem. This synthesis module pulls the threads from across the curriculum into a single bedside algorithm.

After this module you will:

  • Run the 5-question loop in < 30 seconds to localise weakness.
  • Rule out the "do-not-miss" emergencies before history (stroke, cord compression, GBS, MG crisis, botulism, hypokalaemic paralysis, eclampsia).
  • Differentiate UMN, LMN, NMJ, and muscle patterns at the bedside.
  • Choose the right first-tier workup by suspected level (CT/MRI brain, MRI spine, FVC, AChR antibodies, CK, NCS/EMG, electrolytes, B12).
  • Identify functional weakness with rule-in signs (Hoover, hip abductor, drag) and communicate it well.
  • Apply the 20/30/40 rule for neuromuscular respiratory failure (FVC < 20 mL/kg, NIF less negative than −30, MEP < 40).
  • Decide admit vs same-day clinic vs outpatient.
The single principle: tempo + pattern + level usually localises 95% of weakness. Run the loop in 30 seconds, then ask "anything that doesn't fit?" to catch the rare emergency.

Lessons

1. The five-question loop
  1. Is it really weakness — or fatigue, pain, ataxia, apraxia, functional?
  2. Tempo — hyperacute / acute / subacute / chronic / episodic-fatigable?
  3. Pattern — focal / symmetric / proximal / distal / bulbar / fatigable / length-dependent?
  4. Level — UMN / LMN / NMJ / muscle?
  5. Associated — sensory / autonomic / pain / sphincter / bulbar / respiratory / systemic / drugs / family?
Tempo + pattern + level usually localises 95% of weakness.
2. Emergency rule-out checklist
Run before history:
  • Acute spinal cord compression → MRI whole spine + dexamethasone + neurosurgery (Module 10).
  • Stroke → CT/CTA + thrombolysis/thrombectomy (Module 2).
  • GBS respiratory failure → 20/30/40 rule + ICU + IVIG/PLEX (Module 9, 22).
  • MG crisis → PLEX/IVIG + avoid precipitants (Module 9).
  • Botulism → antitoxin / BIG-IV (Module 9).
  • Hypokalaemic paralysis (incl. thyrotoxic) → cautious K + propranolol if thyrotoxic (Module 9, 23).
  • Carotid / vertebral dissection (Module 2, 19).
  • Brainstem stroke (Module 2).
  • Wernicke → thiamine before glucose (Module 13).
  • Hypoglycaemia → glucose (Module 13).
  • Eclampsia / PRES (Module 20).
  • NMS / SS / MH hyperthermia + rigidity (Module 13).

6. NMJ details — MG, LEMS, botulism, drug-induced
  • MG: fatigable bulbar + ocular + proximal; AChR / MuSK / LRP4 antibodies; decremental on RNS; pyridostigmine + steroid + steroid-sparing; thymectomy; modern biologics — eculizumab, efgartigimod (FcRn 2021), rozanolixizumab (2023), zilucoplan (2023); crisis → PLEX / IVIG.
  • LEMS: proximal lower limb improving with brief exercise; dry mouth; reflexes improve after exercise; incremental > 60% on RNS; VGCC; 60% SCLC; 3,4-DAP + treat tumour.
  • Botulism: descending + dilated pupils + dry mouth + bulbar; BIG-IV / equine antitoxin.
  • Drug-induced NMJ block: aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones, magnesium, β-blockers; avoid in MG + caution in pregnancy with eclampsia magnesium (Module 20).

8. Functional weakness rule-in signs
  • Hoover's sign: weak hip extension normalises with contralateral hip flexion against resistance (sensitivity ~63%, specificity ~100%; Stone JNNP 2010).
  • Hip abductor sign (Sonoo); drag / monoplegic gait without circumduction; give-way weakness; inconsistent power.
  • Spinal Injuries Center sign: supine leg won't lift; sitting it lifts effortlessly.
  • Communicate using Stone framework; demonstrate the positive sign; refer to FND-specific physio + CBT (Module 21).
  • Coexistence with neurological disease is common.
9. Systemic / metabolic weakness
  • Electrolytes: hypoK paralysis < 2.5; hypoMg cramps; hypoPO₄ refeeding diaphragm paralysis; hypoCa tetany.
  • Endocrine: thyroid (myxoedema or thyrotoxic); Cushing; vitamin D; hyperPTH; hypoadrenal; pituitary; DM (Module 23).
  • Nutritional: B12 (subacute combined); B1 (Wernicke / dry beriberi); B6; copper; vitamin D; vitamin E (Module 13).
  • Toxic: alcohol; statin; aminoglycoside; isoniazid; vincristine; cisplatin; N₂O; methanol; CO; lead (wrist drop); arsenic; mercury (Module 13).
  • Infection: polio-like (AFM); West Nile; rabies; tetanus; diphtheria; HIV; Lyme; leprosy (Module 11).
  • Paraneoplastic (Module 12): LEMS; anti-Hu; dermatomyositis; SCLC.
  • Rheumatological (Module 23): SLE; Sjögren; vasculitis; sarcoid; RA atlantoaxial.

Case Vignettes

Case 1 — A 68-year-old man with AF develops sudden right hemiparesis + global aphasia at 11:00. NIHSS 18

Diagnosis: Acute ischaemic stroke (left MCA territory).

Plan:

  1. FAST screen + stroke pathway activation (Module 2).
  2. Non-contrast CT + CT angiography immediately to exclude haemorrhage + identify LVO.
  3. IV tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg (AHA/ASA 2026 — Class 1 alternative to alteplase) within 4.5 h if no contraindication.
  4. Mechanical thrombectomy if LVO and within window (up to 24 h with imaging-based selection).
  5. Admit to stroke unit; secondary prevention for AF (DOAC + statin + BP).
  6. Rehabilitation pathway (Module 25): AVERT 2015 short-frequent sessions, avoid very high doses in first 24 h.
Case 2 — A 35-year-old develops ascending weakness over 5 days, now unable to walk, with absent reflexes throughout. FVC 18 mL/kg

Recent Campylobacter gastroenteritis 2 weeks ago.

Diagnosis: Guillain-Barré syndrome (AIDP) with impending respiratory failure (20/30/40 rule met).

Plan:

  1. ICU admission with telemetry — autonomic instability is major mortality driver (Module 15).
  2. Intubate now (FVC < 20). Avoid succinylcholine (hyperkalaemia from upregulated AChRs).
  3. IVIG 0.4 g/kg/day × 5 days or plasma exchange × 5 sessions (equivalent).
  4. LP: albuminocytologic dissociation (high protein, normal cells).
  5. NCS may be normal in first week.
  6. VTE prophylaxis (LMWH).
  7. Rehabilitation planning (Module 25): gradual physiotherapy as strength returns; 6–12 months recovery; ~80% reach independent ambulation.
  8. Cross-ref Modules 9, 22.
Case 3 — A 60-year-old with breast cancer on chemo develops 2 weeks of progressive bilateral leg weakness, T6 sensory level, and urinary retention. Severe back pain

Diagnosis: Metastatic epidural spinal cord compression (MESCC) until proven otherwise.

Plan within 6 hours:

  1. Dexamethasone 10 mg IV bolus then 4 mg IV q6h (or 16 mg/day in divided doses).
  2. Urgent whole-spine MRI (not just symptomatic level — multilevel disease common).
  3. Combined neurosurgery + radiation oncology consultation.
  4. If ambulatory, single level, life expectancy > 3 months: surgical decompression + RT (Patchell trial).
  5. Bladder catheter; VTE prophylaxis; analgesia; bowel regimen.
  6. Restage systemic disease; tumour board discussion of systemic therapy.
  7. Cross-ref Modules 10, 12.
Case 4 — A 28-year-old East Asian man develops sudden bilateral leg paralysis after a large carbohydrate meal followed by sleep. He has been losing weight + tremulous. K 1.8

Eye signs: lid lag. Mild proptosis. Pulse 120 with mild atrial fibrillation.

Diagnosis: Thyrotoxic hypokalaemic periodic paralysis.

Plan:

  1. Cautious K replacement — typically 10 mmol/h IV with cardiac monitoring (risk of rebound hyperkalaemia as K shifts back out of cells).
  2. Propranolol 1–3 mg IV slowly (or oral 60–80 mg) — addresses underlying thyrotoxic effect, prevents shifts, restores K.
  3. TSH + free T4/T3 to confirm thyrotoxicosis; treat thyrotoxicosis (carbimazole / methimazole + later definitive radioiodine or thyroidectomy).
  4. Avoid further triggers (high-carbohydrate meals, alcohol, stress, vigorous exercise).
  5. Cross-ref Modules 9, 23.
Case 5 — A 22-year-old woman has 3 months of unexplained left leg weakness with give-way pattern + drag gait. MRI brain and spine normal. NCS normal. Hoover's sign positive

No precipitating event. No sensory complaints. Recently bereaved.

Diagnosis: Functional weakness (left leg).

Plan:

  1. Communicate the diagnosis using the Stone framework:
    • Name it ("functional neurological disorder").
    • Explain the mechanism ("hardware intact, software stuck in faulty pattern").
    • Demonstrate the positive Hoover's sign to the patient.
    • Differentiate from feigning ("not under your conscious control").
    • Explain normal investigations.
    • Plan with hope (FND-specific physiotherapy + CBT).
  2. Refer to FND-specific physiotherapy (Nielsen 2015 consensus — distract-attention strategies, automatic over effortful movement, avoid splints/aids beyond minimum).
  3. Address comorbid depression / bereavement; sleep; pain.
  4. Avoid further unnecessary investigations once diagnosis is secure.
  5. Patient resource: neurosymptoms.org.
  6. Cross-ref Module 21.
Case 6 — A 55-year-old man has 9 months of progressive limb weakness with both UMN signs (brisk reflexes, Babinski) and LMN signs (fasciculations, wasting), bulbar features (dysarthria + dysphagia), no sensory involvement, no sphincter problems

FVC 70%. No family history.

Diagnosis: Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) — combined UMN + LMN in multiple regions without sensory.

Plan:

  1. Confirm with EMG (Awaji criteria) — denervation + reinnervation across multiple regions.
  2. Genetic counselling — C9orf72, SOD1, TARDBP, FUS especially with bulbar onset / family history.
  3. Riluzole 50 mg BD + edaravone (FDA 2017, IV / oral) where available. AMX0035 withdrawn 2024. Tofersen for SOD1-ALS (FDA 2023).
  4. Multidisciplinary ALS clinic improves survival + QoL (Module 9, 25). Coordinate neurology + nursing + SLT + PT + OT + dietitian + respiratory + social work + palliative.
  5. Respiratory: PFT + SNIP + NIV at FVC < 50% predicted or symptomatic — improves survival + QoL.
  6. Bulbar: SLT for dysarthria + dysphagia + voice banking + AAC (eye-gaze, BCIs in selected); PEG before FVC drops too low (Module 9).
  7. Spasticity: baclofen; sialorrhoea: glycopyrrolate / atropine drops / BoNT to salivary glands; cramps: gabapentin / quinine; pseudobulbar affect: dextromethorphan-quinidine.
  8. Early palliative care integration + advance care planning + hospice + bereavement.
  9. Cross-ref Modules 9, 25.
