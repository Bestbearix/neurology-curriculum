Module 27 Synthesis

Approach to the Comatose Patient

A cross-module synthesis. The four-pillar exam, anatomical localisation, the 10-second reversible-cause sweep, and structured workup — drawing on Modules 2, 3, 5, 11, 13, 18, 19, 22.

0. Why this module?

Coma is the highest-stakes neurological presentation. Every minute matters: hypoglycaemia kills cortex, herniation kills brainstem, status epilepticus kills neurons, raised ICP irreversibly distorts midline structures. A structured approach — pillar exam → anatomical localisation → reversible-cause sweep — converts a sea of possibilities into a treatable plan inside the first 10 minutes.

One principle: resuscitate while you localise. ABC + glucose + thiamine + naloxone come before any imaging.

Arousal lives in the ascending reticular activating system (ARAS) from upper pons → midbrain → thalamus → cortex. Damage anywhere on that axis, OR diffuse bihemispheric cortical dysfunction, produces coma.

2. The first 10 minutes — resuscitate while you localise

  1. Airway — protect; intubate if GCS ≤ 8 or no gag.
  2. Breathing — pulse ox, ABG; if hyperventilating consider acidosis/sepsis/Kussmaul; if hypoventilating consider opioid/sedative/CO₂ narcosis.
  3. Circulation — BP, HR; treat cardiogenic shock; Cushing's triad (hypertension + bradycardia + irregular respiration) = raised ICP.
  4. Dextrose — finger-stick glucose. Hypoglycaemia must be ruled out before any imaging.
  5. Empirical cocktail when relevant:
    • Thiamine 500 mg IV (before glucose, as in Wernicke prevention — Module 13).
    • Glucose if hypoglycaemic (D50W 50 mL adult; D25W 2 mL/kg paeds).
    • Naloxone 0.04–2 mg IV titrated to respiratory rate (fentanyl needs higher doses and infusions — Module 13).
    • Flumazenil only if isolated iatrogenic benzodiazepine sedation (AVOID if mixed overdose / chronic benzo use → can precipitate withdrawal seizures).
  6. Bloods: glucose, lactate, ABG, U&E, LFT, NH₃, ethanol, paracetamol, salicylate, troponin, FBC, coag, CK, blood cultures, β-hCG, drug screen.
  7. Empirical antibiotics + aciclovir + dexamethasone if meningitis/encephalitis is in the differential and CT-before-LP indications apply (Module 11) — antibiotics within 60 min.
  8. Treat seizures empirically if any suggestion of NCSE (eye deviation, twitching, fluctuating mental state) — IV lorazepam 4 mg, then second-line (Module 3).
DO NOT delay glucose for imaging. DO NOT give glucose without thiamine in chronic alcohol use, hyperemesis, malnutrition, bariatric, prolonged TPN (Modules 13, 20).

3. The four-pillar exam (Plum & Posner)

The bedside exam answers one question: is the brainstem intact, or is the coma structural?

1. Level of consciousnessGCS, FOUR scoreTrajectory matters as much as snapshot
2. PupilsSize, symmetry, reactivity (Module 19)Localises rostral-caudal level + drug clues
3. Eye movements + brainstem reflexesSpontaneous gaze, dolls-eyes (oculocephalic), cold caloric (oculovestibular), corneals, cough/gagTests pons–midbrain integrity

Use GCS (3–15) AND FOUR score (0–16; eye, motor, brainstem, respiration; better in intubated). Trend serial scores.

Eye movements + brainstem reflexes

  • Oculocephalic (dolls-eyes): passive head rotation drives conjugate gaze opposite. Requires intact pons-midbrain MLF. Do NOT perform if cervical injury suspected.
  • Oculovestibular (cold caloric): ice-water 30 mL into ear with intact tympanic membrane. Normal awake → fast-phase nystagmus AWAY from cold (mnemonic COWS — Cold Opposite, Warm Same). In coma with intact brainstem → tonic deviation TOWARDS cold. Absent → brainstem damage or drug effect.
  • Corneal: CN V → VII (pontine).
  • Cough / gag: CN IX, X (medullary).
  • Skew deviation = brainstem/cerebellar.
  • Roving conjugate eye movements = brainstem intact.
  • Ocular bobbing = pontine.
  • One-and-a-half syndrome = pontine (PPRF + MLF).

Rostral-caudal deterioration (diencephalic → midbrain → pontine → medullary signs in sequence) suggests progressive transtentorial herniation. Pupils + motor + respiration deteriorate together.

4. Anatomical localisation — coma is one of three things

  1. Bilateral cortical / hemispheric — usually toxic-metabolic; structural rare (bilateral massive strokes, anoxic injury, SAH, encephalitis, status epilepticus, NCSE).
  2. Bilateral thalamic / diencephalic — top-of-basilar embolism (artery of Percheron), thalamic haemorrhage, central herniation.
  3. Brainstem (upper pons → midbrain) — basilar artery thrombosis, brainstem haemorrhage, central pontine myelinolysis (Module 13), tumour, MS plaque, basilar migraine.

Add herniation as a mechanism: uncal (lateral mass → CN III + ipsilateral hemiparesis or Kernohan notch), central (rostral-caudal), tonsillar (posterior fossa → respiratory arrest), subfalcine (ACA infarct).

6. The CT decision and imaging algorithm

After ABC + glucose + reversible-cause cocktail:

  1. Non-contrast CT head within minutes — ICH, SAH, mass, hydrocephalus, midline shift, large infarct.
  2. CT angiography if basilar / LVO suspected (sudden onset + brainstem findings + crossed signs / "locked-in").
  3. MRI brain within hours if CT non-diagnostic — DWI for early infarct (especially posterior fossa), encephalitis, anoxic injury, top-of-basilar.
  4. EEG if NCSE suspected (Module 3): 30% of unexplained coma in ICU has NCSE on continuous EEG.
  5. LP after CT if meningitis/encephalitis suspected — DO NOT delay antibiotics + dexamethasone + aciclovir for LP (Module 11).
  6. Toxicology / ABG / co-oximetry if toxic suspected — co-oximetry detects CO poisoning (standard SpO₂ misleadingly normal).

8. Raised ICP management (Module 22)

  1. Head elevation 30°; neutral neck; avoid jugular obstruction.
  2. Adequate sedation + analgesia; treat fever (every 1°C ↑ ICP).
  3. Osmotherapy: 3% hypertonic saline 250 mL bolus or mannitol 1 g/kg; target Na 145–155 or osmolality < 320.
  4. Avoid hyper- and hypoglycaemia; PaCO₂ 35–40 mmHg (transient hyperventilation to 30–35 for impending herniation only).
  5. External ventricular drain if hydrocephalus.
  6. Decompressive craniectomy for malignant MCA infarct (DECIMAL/DESTINY/HAMLET pooled — Module 2) or refractory raised ICP after TBI (RESCUEicp 2016 — Module 22).
  7. Anticoagulation reversal: warfarin → 4F-PCC + vitamin K; dabigatran → idarucizumab; factor Xa inhibitors → andexanet alfa (or 4F-PCC if andexanet unavailable).

9. Hypoxic-ischaemic encephalopathy (post-arrest) — Module 13, 22

  • Targeted temperature management 32–36 °C for 24 h after ROSC (HACA 2002, TTM 2013, TTM2 2021 — normothermia 36 °C non-inferior to 33 °C in TTM2).
  • Avoid hyperthermia post-arrest at all costs.
  • Multimodal prognostication after ≥ 72 h off sedation — combine ≥ 2 of: bilateral absent N20 SSEPs, status myoclonus day 1, NSE > 60 ng/mL at 48–72 h, EEG burst-suppression or status without reactivity, MRI DWI showing cytotoxic oedema. NEVER prognosticate on a single test (especially < 72 h).
  • Lance-Adams syndrome (action myoclonus weeks after arrest in awake survivors) — clonazepam, valproate.
  • Delayed post-hypoxic leukoencephalopathy 1–4 weeks later.

10. Brain death determination (Module 5)

Wijdicks AAN 2010 framework:

  1. Prerequisites: known irreversible catastrophic cause; exclude confounders (drugs, hypothermia < 36 °C, metabolic, neuromuscular blockade); SBP ≥ 100; core temperature ≥ 36 °C.
  2. Coma + absent brainstem reflexes: pupils fixed; no corneal, no oculocephalic, no caloric, no gag, no cough; no motor to noxious cranial nerve stimulation.
  3. Apnoea test: pre-oxygenate; disconnect; observe 8–10 min; ABG. Test positive if no respiratory effort and PaCO₂ ≥ 60 mmHg (or ≥ 20 mmHg above baseline). Abort if SBP < 90, SpO₂ < 85%, arrhythmia.
  4. Ancillary tests if exam/apnoea cannot be completed: cerebral angiography (gold standard), EEG, nuclear flow scan, transcranial Doppler.
  5. Spinal reflexes may persist (Lazarus sign, triple flexion) and do NOT preclude brain death.

11. Prognostic frameworks beyond HIE

  • TBI: IMPACT and CRASH calculators; age + motor + pupils + CT (Marshall / Rotterdam).
  • ICH: ICH score (GCS, age, ICH volume, IVH, infratentorial).
  • SAH: Hunt-Hess, WFNS, modified Fisher.
  • Stroke: NIHSS; mRS trajectory; large core not always doomed (DAWN, DEFUSE 3 — Module 2).
  • Anti-NMDA-R encephalitis: prolonged ICU but most recover with treatment (Module 11).

12. Disorders of consciousness — recovery and rehabilitation (Module 25)

  • Use Coma Recovery Scale-Revised (CRS-R) to distinguish UWS, MCS−, MCS+, EMCS.
  • Up to 40% of "vegetative" patients reclassify as MCS on careful CRS-R; covert cognition demonstrated by fMRI / EEG (Owen, Monti).
  • Amantadine 200 → 400 mg/day (Giacino NEJM 2012) improves recovery in TBI 4–16 weeks post-injury.
  • Early intensive multidisciplinary rehabilitation; tracheostomy + PEG timing; spasticity management; sialorrhoea; sleep-wake regulation.
  • Family communication is essential — refrain from absolute statements before 72 h prognostication window.

14. When to refer / escalate

  • Any coma with GCS ≤ 8 → intubate + ICU.
  • Suspected basilar artery occlusion → neurointervention IMMEDIATELY (extended thrombectomy windows — Module 2).
  • Suspected SAH → neurosurgery + interventional neuroradiology for coiling/clipping.
  • Suspected MESCC with descending coma from cervical level → neurosurgery (Module 10).
  • NCSE refractory to first + second line → neurology + ICU for anaesthetic infusion (Module 3).
  • Suspected brain death → trained certifiers per local protocol; family + ethics + organ donation pathway.
  • Disorders of consciousness > 28 days → DOC specialist + CRS-R serial + amantadine consideration (Module 25).
