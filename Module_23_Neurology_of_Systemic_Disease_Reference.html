1. Framework — the body is the brain's environment

The brain is exquisitely sensitive to systemic disease. Every primary internal medicine problem — heart failure, AKI, hyperthyroidism, sickle cell, lupus, coeliac disease, HIV, organ transplantation — can present as a neurological syndrome before the systemic diagnosis is suspected. This module organises neurological complications by organ system. The principle: ask what the patient's organs are doing every time you see a confused, weak, ataxic, or seizing patient — many "primary neurology" presentations turn out to be the cardiac arrhythmia, the thyroid storm, the antiphospholipid syndrome, or the lupus flare.

The single principle: in any new neurological syndrome, do a systems review and basic systemic workup. Many neurological emergencies (TTP, GCA, eclampsia, lupus cerebritis, antiphospholipid catastrophic syndrome, sickle cell stroke, IE-related cerebral embolus, hepatic encephalopathy) declare themselves only when the systemic clues are sought.

2. Cardiac disease and the brain

Cardioembolic stroke

  • Atrial fibrillation: ~5× increased stroke risk; strokes tend to be larger and more disabling than non-cardioembolic. CHA₂DS₂-VASc guides anticoagulation. Modern preference: DOAC (apixaban, rivaroxaban, dabigatran, edoxaban) over warfarin except in mechanical valves (warfarin) and rheumatic mitral stenosis (warfarin). Cross-ref Module 2.
  • Acute MI / LV thrombus: anticoagulate for 3 months minimum.
  • Atrial myxoma: rare; cardiac echo; surgery.
  • Patent foramen ovale (PFO): paradoxical embolus in cryptogenic stroke in young patients (RoPE score). Three RCTs (CLOSE, REDUCE, RESPECT extended follow-up) showed reduced stroke recurrence with PFO closure in selected patients < 60 years with high-risk PFO features.
  • Cardiac surgery: peri-operative stroke (~2% in CABG; higher in valve surgery + redo); post-pump cognitive decline; perioperative AF.

Infective endocarditis (IE)

  • Up to 35% of patients with IE develop neurological complications.
  • Septic embolic stroke (commonest), mycotic aneurysm (distal MCA branches; risk of haemorrhage 5%), brain abscess, meningitis, encephalopathy.
  • Anticoagulation in IE is controversial — increases ICH risk; usually held in active IE with neurological involvement; restart after stabilisation.
  • Cardiac surgery timing: early surgery (within days) is reasonable for ischaemic stroke without haemorrhagic conversion or major neurological deficit; delay 2–4 weeks for haemorrhagic complications.
  • Cross-ref Module 11 (CNS infection differential).

Cardiac arrest and hypoxic-ischaemic encephalopathy

See Module 22 — post-cardiac-arrest care and multimodal neuroprognostication ≥ 72 hours; TTM2 targeted normothermia.

Takotsubo cardiomyopathy and neurogenic stress

  • "Apical ballooning"; precipitated by severe emotional or physical stress, severe acute neurological insult (especially SAH and ICH).
  • Catecholamine-mediated myocardial stunning; LV apical ballooning; ST changes mimic MI; troponin moderate.
  • Usually reverses in 1–4 weeks; supportive ICU care.

Heart failure and the brain

  • Low-output state → cognitive impairment, increased dementia risk; depression common.
  • Cardio-renal-cerebral interactions; manage HF, treat sleep apnea (Module 14 — Cheyne-Stokes in HFrEF; ASV contraindicated if EF ≤ 45 per SERVE-HF), avoid anticholinergics, screen for cognitive decline.

3. Renal disease, dialysis, electrolytes

Uraemic encephalopathy (Module 13)

  • GFR usually < 10 mL/min; cognitive slowing → asterixis, multifocal myoclonus, seizures, coma.
  • EEG: diffuse slowing ± triphasic waves. BUN/urea correlates more closely than creatinine.
  • Reverses with dialysis or transplant over days.

Dialysis disequilibrium syndrome

  • Headache, nausea, seizures, encephalopathy during or just after dialysis (especially first dialysis with high BUN).
  • Aggressive urea clearance produces an osmotic gradient → cerebral oedema.
  • Prevent with short gentle initial sessions; treat with mannitol if severe.

Dialysis dementia (now rare)

Subacute progressive dementia, dysarthria, myoclonus, seizures from aluminium accumulation. Disappeared after removal of aluminium from dialysate.

Renal transplant neurology

  • Calcineurin inhibitor (tacrolimus, ciclosporin) neurotoxicity: tremor, PRES (Module 13), seizures, neuropathy.
  • Opportunistic infections (PML, Listeria, CMV, toxoplasmosis); reactivation of HBV/HCV.
  • Post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder with CNS involvement.
  • Manage electrolytes, BP carefully; avoid drug interactions.

Electrolyte and osmolar disturbances (Module 13)

Hyponatraemia (acute: seizures, coma; correct ≤ 8 mmol/L/24 h to avoid CPM); hypernatraemia (lethargy, coma; risk of ICH in severe); hypercalcaemia (drowsiness, confusion, psychosis); hypocalcaemia (tetany, seizures, prolonged QT); hypomagnesaemia (refractory hypocalcaemia/hypokalaemia, seizures); hypoglycaemia and hyperglycaemia; hyperammonaemia (also valproate, urea-cycle defects, post-bariatric).

4. Endocrine disease

Thyroid disease

ConditionNeurology
ThyrotoxicosisPostural tremor, proximal myopathy, anxiety, irritability, sleep disturbance, periodic paralysis (thyrotoxic — especially East Asian men with hypokalaemia), thyroid storm (delirium, fever, tachycardia, agitation, multi-organ failure); ophthalmopathy (Graves — IGF-1R antibodies; teprotumumab FDA 2020 — Module 19); thyroid eye disease + optic nerve compression.
MyxoedemaCognitive slowing, depression, ataxia (Hashimoto cerebellar ataxia), proximal myopathy with elevated CK, carpal tunnel; myxoedema coma with hypothermia + bradycardia + altered mental status — IV levothyroxine + hydrocortisone + supportive ICU; Hashimoto encephalopathy (steroid-responsive encephalopathy associated with autoimmune thyroiditis — SREAT) with seizures, confusion, myoclonus, anti-TPO antibodies, dramatic steroid response.

Adrenal disease

  • Addison disease / adrenal crisis: lethargy → coma, hyperpigmentation, hyponatraemia, hyperkalaemia, hypoglycaemia, hypotension; treat with hydrocortisone 100 mg IV stat + fluids; adrenoleukodystrophy (X-linked, VLCFA elevated) can present with adrenal insufficiency in young men.
  • Cushing syndrome: cognitive impairment, depression, psychosis, proximal myopathy, hippocampal atrophy.
  • Phaeochromocytoma: paroxysmal hypertension with headache + sweating + palpitations (Module 15). α-blockade BEFORE β-blockade — avoid unopposed α-vasoconstriction.

Pituitary (cross-ref Module 12)

  • Macroadenoma — bitemporal hemianopia; hormone excess (prolactinoma, GH, ACTH, TSH) or hypopituitarism; pituitary apoplexy = emergency (hydrocortisone 100 mg IV stat).
  • Sheehan syndrome — postpartum pituitary necrosis (Module 20).

Diabetes mellitus

  • Cerebrovascular: large vessel disease, lacunar stroke, silent infarcts; control glycaemia + CV risk (Module 2).
  • Neuropathies: length-dependent symmetric (Module 9); diabetic autonomic neuropathy (Module 15); diabetic amyotrophy (lumbosacral radiculoplexus neuropathy — painful thigh wasting); third nerve palsy usually pupil-sparing (Module 19); mononeuropathies (CTS, ulnar, peroneal).
  • Hypoglycaemia unawareness: from cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy (Module 15).
  • Hyperosmolar hyperglycaemic state: focal motor seizures characteristic — resolve with glucose normalisation, not ASMs (Module 13).
  • Diabetic ketoacidosis: cerebral oedema in children — emergency.

Parathyroid

  • Hyperparathyroidism: hypercalcaemia neurology (depression, fatigue, confusion, proximal weakness, peptic ulcer, renal stones — "stones, bones, abdominal moans, psychic groans").
  • Hypoparathyroidism: hypocalcaemia neurology (tetany, Chvostek, Trousseau, seizures, basal ganglia calcification — Fahr disease).

5. Haematological disease

Sickle cell disease

  • ~10% of children with sickle cell have a clinical stroke by age 20 if untreated.
  • Transcranial Doppler screening annually from age 2 (STOP trial, Adams NEJM 1998); TCD velocity > 200 cm/s indicates high stroke risk.
  • Primary prevention: chronic transfusion to keep HbS < 30% — substantially reduces first stroke. Hydroxyurea also reduces stroke risk and many programmes have replaced indefinite transfusion with hydroxyurea after stabilisation (TWiTCH trial 2016).
  • Acute stroke: exchange transfusion + standard stroke care; cross-ref Module 2.
  • Bone marrow transplant for selected severe cases.
  • Silent cerebral infarcts cause cognitive impairment even without overt stroke.

Polycythaemia, leukaemia, thrombocytopenia

  • Polycythaemia vera (high haematocrit + JAK2 V617F): stroke risk increased; venesection to keep haematocrit < 45%.
  • Leukaemia: leptomeningeal infiltration (cranial neuropathies, headache, cauda equina), CNS involvement of ALL (intrathecal methotrexate); hyperviscosity (AML blast crisis with WBC > 100,000); chemotherapy neurotoxicity (vincristine neuropathy, methotrexate leukoencephalopathy, ifosfamide encephalopathy).
  • Thrombocytopenia and bleeding: ICH risk increases at platelet < 20–50; transfusion thresholds vary by clinical context.

TTP, HUS, DIC

  • Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP): classic pentad (only in 40%) — microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia (schistocytes) + thrombocytopenia + fever + renal injury + neurological symptoms (confusion, seizures, focal deficit, headache). ADAMTS13 deficiency (acquired antibody more common than congenital). Emergency: plasma exchange daily until response; caplacizumab (anti-vWF nanobody — TITAN/HERCULES trials NEJM) reduces relapse + time to response; corticosteroids; rituximab for refractory.
  • HUS: shiga-toxin–producing E. coli; supportive care; eculizumab for atypical HUS.
  • DIC: ICH risk; treat underlying cause; replace components.

Antiphospholipid syndrome

  • Venous + arterial thromboses; recurrent fetal loss; livedo reticularis; thrombocytopenia.
  • Lupus anticoagulant + anti-cardiolipin + anti-β2-glycoprotein-I (Sapporo criteria).
  • Catastrophic antiphospholipid syndrome (CAPS): multi-organ thrombosis with high mortality; treat with anticoagulation + corticosteroid + plasma exchange / IVIG.
  • Anticoagulation: warfarin INR 2–3 (or higher for arterial / recurrent); DOACs less effective than warfarin in APS (TRAPS trial — rivaroxaban inferior; AAN/EAN guidance avoid DOAC in APS especially "triple-positive").
  • Cross-ref Module 2 stroke recurrence prevention.

Paraneoplastic and immune haematological causes

Paraneoplastic syndromes (Module 12), POEMS syndrome (Polyneuropathy, Organomegaly, Endocrinopathy, M-protein, Skin), Waldenström macroglobulinaemia hyperviscosity, multiple myeloma (cord compression — Module 10), MGUS-associated neuropathy.

6. Hepatic disease (cross-ref Module 13)

Hepatic encephalopathy (West Haven; HEPATICS precipitants; lactulose + rifaximin; titrate to 2–3 soft stools/day, not ammonia); Wilson disease (KF rings; ATP7B; trientine preferred over penicillamine in neurological; zinc; Module 13); acquired hepatocerebral degeneration; alcohol-related neurology (Wernicke-Korsakoff; cerebellar degeneration; Marchiafava-Bignami; CPM/ODS).

7. Rheumatological / autoimmune disease

Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE)

  • "Neuropsychiatric SLE" (ACR 19 syndromes): cognitive dysfunction, depression, mood disorder, psychosis, seizures, stroke (often APS-mediated), demyelinating syndrome, aseptic meningitis, transverse myelitis, headache, chorea (especially with APS), mononeuritis multiplex.
  • Workup: ANA, ds-DNA, complement (C3/C4 — low in active flare), APS antibodies; MRI brain; LP for cells, OCBs.
  • Treat lupus + immunosuppression (high-dose steroid, IV cyclophosphamide for severe CNS lupus, mycophenolate, rituximab); anti-APS thrombosis with anticoagulation.

Sjögren syndrome

  • Sicca + small-fibre / autonomic neuropathy + sensory ganglionopathy (anti-Hu-like but not paraneoplastic) + cranial neuropathies (V) + transverse myelitis / NMO-like + cognitive impairment.
  • Anti-Ro/SSA + anti-La/SSB; minor salivary gland biopsy.
  • Treat with immunosuppression (cyclophosphamide for severe CNS); rituximab.

Rheumatoid arthritis

  • Cervical myelopathy from atlantoaxial subluxation — pannus erodes transverse ligament → C1-2 instability; high-risk surgical patient (intubation, anaesthesia positioning). Pre-op flexion/extension cervical spine films or MRI.
  • Entrapment neuropathies (CTS, ulnar at elbow); rheumatoid vasculitis with mononeuritis multiplex.

Sarcoidosis

  • Neurosarcoidosis in ~5% of sarcoidosis patients. Cranial neuropathies (facial — Bell-like; optic neuritis); chronic basal meningitis; hypothalamic / pituitary involvement (diabetes insipidus); myelopathy; peripheral neuropathy; muscle involvement.
  • MRI: leptomeningeal enhancement particularly basal; spinal cord involvement.
  • CSF: lymphocytic pleocytosis, elevated protein, low glucose mimicking TB or fungal (Module 11).
  • Workup: ACE (raised but non-specific); chest CT for hilar adenopathy; PET-CT; tissue biopsy (transbronchial, lymph node, skin).
  • Treatment: corticosteroid; methotrexate, mycophenolate, infliximab (TNF-α) for refractory.

Behçet disease

  • Recurrent oral + genital ulcers + uveitis + skin lesions.
  • Neuro-Behçet: brainstem syndrome (parenchymal) and CVST (vascular); MRI brainstem T2 hyperintensity; CSF inflammation.
  • Treatment: steroid + immunosuppression (cyclophosphamide, azathioprine, biologics — infliximab, adalimumab).

IgG4-related disease

Hypertrophic pachymeningitis; orbital pseudotumour; hypophysitis; cranial neuropathies; mimics neurosarcoidosis. Serum IgG4 + biopsy; corticosteroid + rituximab.

Connective tissue overlap and other rheumatological

Mixed connective tissue disease (Raynaud + sclerodactyly + polymyositis); systemic sclerosis (myopathy; rarely CNS); polymyositis / dermatomyositis (Module 9); anti-synthetase syndrome.

8. Gastrointestinal disease

  • Coeliac disease: peripheral neuropathy, gluten ataxia (anti-gliadin / TG6 / TG2 antibodies; cerebellar atrophy; gluten-free diet), epilepsy with bilateral occipital calcifications, B12 deficiency (Module 13), vitamin E deficiency, peripheral neuropathy. Always check coeliac serology in unexplained ataxia or neuropathy.
  • Inflammatory bowel disease: thromboembolic stroke (hypercoagulable state); CVST; CNS demyelination (TNF inhibitors can unmask MS — switch DMT); peripheral neuropathy (vitamin B12 deficiency after ileal resection — Module 13).
  • Bariatric surgery: Wernicke encephalopathy from thiamine deficiency (Module 13, 20); B12 deficiency; copper deficiency myeloneuropathy; vitamin D / calcium / vitamin E deficiencies.
  • Whipple disease (Tropheryma whipplei): malabsorption + arthralgia + neurological (oculomasticatory myorhythmia is pathognomonic — slow rhythmic ocular convergence-divergence with synchronous masseter contraction); ataxia, dementia; PCR + jejunal biopsy; ceftriaxone + TMP-SMX long-term.

9. Respiratory disease

  • Sleep apnea (Module 14): stroke / AF / HTN / cognitive impairment; CPAP improves daytime symptoms and BP.
  • CO₂ narcosis (Module 13): chronic hypercapnia → asterixis, drowsiness, papilloedema.
  • Hypoxia / pulmonary embolus: cerebral hypoxia in massive PE.
  • Pulmonary sarcoidosis: see above for neurosarcoidosis.
  • Respiratory failure in neuromuscular disease: GBS, MG, ALS, muscular dystrophy — Module 9. 20/30/40 rule and NIV.

10. HIV and neurosyphilis (cross-ref Module 11)

  • HIV-associated neurocognitive disorders (HAND): asymptomatic neurocognitive impairment, mild NCD, HIV-associated dementia; ART reduces incidence; cognitive screening; treat opportunistic infections.
  • Opportunistic infections in HIV (Module 11): cerebral toxoplasmosis (multiple ring-enhancing in basal ganglia / grey-white junction; pyrimethamine + sulfadiazine + leucovorin); primary CNS lymphoma (single periventricular; defer steroids; high-dose methotrexate); cryptococcal meningitis (AMBITION-cm 2022 single-dose liposomal amphotericin B + flucytosine + fluconazole); PML (JCV); CMV ventriculitis; CNS TB.
  • Immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS): paradoxical worsening after ART initiation; manage with steroids.
  • Neurosyphilis: tabes dorsalis (lightning pain, ataxia, Argyll-Robertson pupils, areflexia, Charcot joints), general paresis (dementia, psychosis), meningovascular syphilis (stroke in young patients), gummata. Treat with IV penicillin G 18–24 MU daily × 14 days.

11. Solid organ transplantation

  • Calcineurin inhibitor neurotoxicity (tacrolimus, ciclosporin): tremor, headache, encephalopathy, seizures, PRES (Module 13), peripheral neuropathy. Reduce or rotate; switch to mTOR inhibitor (sirolimus, everolimus) or belatacept.
  • Opportunistic infections: CMV, PML (JCV), Listeria, Nocardia, toxoplasmosis, fungal, TB; check IgG status and prophylaxis (TMP-SMX for PCP/Toxo; valganciclovir for CMV).
  • Post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder (PTLD): EBV-driven; CNS involvement; reduce immunosuppression; rituximab; chemotherapy if needed.
  • Neuropathies: critical-illness PN (Module 22); peri-operative compression; uraemic / hepatic neuropathy pre-transplant.
  • Centrally pontine myelinolysis (osmotic demyelination): after rapid sodium correction (Module 13).
  • Bone marrow transplant: graft-versus-host disease (CNS rare); CNS infections; chemotherapy / radiation effects.

12. Radiation injury

  • Acute (during / immediately after RT): tumour-related symptoms worsen from oedema → steroids.
  • Early-delayed (1–6 months): somnolence syndrome; transient demyelination; usually reversible.
  • Late-delayed (months to years): radiation necrosis (mimics tumour recurrence — MR perfusion/spectroscopy + PET help differentiate; bevacizumab often used; resection in selected); leukoencephalopathy with cognitive decline; cavernous malformations developing decades later; radiation-induced tumour (meningioma, glioma after decades); endocrinopathy if pituitary in field; cranial neuropathies.
  • Spinal radiation myelopathy: progressive myelopathy at irradiated cord segment; difficult to treat; bevacizumab + supportive.
  • Radiation plexopathy: brachial / lumbosacral — slowly progressive, painless (vs malignant infiltration which is painful); myokymia on EMG suggests radiation.

13. When to refer

  • Same day: any new neurological syndrome with systemic features suggesting catastrophic antiphospholipid syndrome / TTP / GCA with visual loss / lupus cerebritis / adrenal crisis / myxoedema coma / thyroid storm / DKA cerebral oedema / suspected cardiac source of embolus with active stroke.
  • Within 1–2 weeks: new SLE / Sjögren / vasculitis workup for CNS involvement; suspected neurosarcoidosis; HIV-positive patient with new neurological symptoms; transplant patient with new neurological symptoms.
  • Routine: established cardiac, renal, endocrine, haematological, autoimmune conditions with stable neurological complications; chronic radiation effects.
  • Always involve: relevant subspecialist (cardiology, nephrology, endocrinology, haematology, rheumatology, gastroenterology, ID, transplant team, radiation oncology, ophthalmology) plus neurology; multidisciplinary planning is essential.
