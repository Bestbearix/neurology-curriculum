Why this module?

Coma is the highest-stakes neurological presentation. A structured first 10 minutes — ABC + glucose + thiamine + naloxone, then a four-pillar exam, then targeted imaging and EEG — converts a sea of possibilities into a treatable plan.

After this module you will:

  • Run the empirical resuscitation cocktail before any imaging.
  • Perform the four-pillar Plum & Posner exam (LOC + pupils + eyes/brainstem + motor/respiration).
  • Localise coma to bilateral cortical, bilateral thalamic, or brainstem mechanisms.
  • Run the AEIOU-TIPS reversible-cause sweep in 10 seconds.
  • Decide CT vs MRI vs continuous EEG vs LP.
  • Identify coma mimics (locked-in, akinetic mutism, catatonia, NCSE, functional).
  • Apply Wijdicks 2010 brain death criteria correctly.
  • Use multimodal prognostication at ≥ 72 h for HIE — never on a single test.
The single principle: resuscitate while you localise. ABC + glucose + thiamine + naloxone come before any imaging. Hypoglycaemia kills cortex faster than herniation.

Arousal lives in the ARAS (pons → midbrain → thalamus → cortex). Damage anywhere on that axis OR diffuse bihemispheric cortical dysfunction = coma.
2. The first 10 minutes — resuscitate while you localise
  1. ABC — intubate if GCS ≤ 8.
  2. Glucose — finger stick BEFORE anything else.
  3. Thiamine 500 mg IV BEFORE glucose if any nutritional risk.
  4. Naloxone 0.04–2 mg IV titrated to respiration (fentanyl needs higher doses + infusion).
  5. Flumazenil ONLY for isolated iatrogenic benzodiazepine — avoid in mixed overdose / chronic use (seizures).
  6. Bloods: glucose, lactate, ABG with co-oximetry (CO!), U&E, LFT, NH₃, ethanol, paracetamol, salicylate, troponin, FBC, coag, CK, blood cultures, β-hCG, drug screen.
  7. Empirical antibiotics + dexamethasone + aciclovir within 60 min if meningitis/encephalitis (Module 11) — do not delay for LP.
  8. Empirical seizure treatment if any suggestion of NCSE (Module 3): lorazepam 4 mg IV.
DO NOT delay glucose for imaging. DO NOT give glucose without thiamine in chronic alcohol use, hyperemesis, malnutrition, bariatric, prolonged TPN.

5. Eye movements + brainstem reflexes
  • Oculocephalic (dolls-eyes): passive head rotation drives conjugate gaze opposite — intact pons-midbrain MLF. NOT if cervical injury.
  • Oculovestibular (cold caloric): ice water 30 mL. In awake → fast-phase AWAY (COWS). In coma with intact brainstem → tonic deviation TOWARDS cold. Absent → brainstem damage or drug.
  • Corneal = CN V → VII (pontine).
  • Cough / gag = CN IX, X (medullary).
  • Roving conjugate = brainstem intact.
  • Ocular bobbing = pontine.
  • One-and-a-half syndrome = pontine (PPRF + MLF).

8. Brain death (Wijdicks AAN 2010)
  1. Prerequisites: known irreversible catastrophic cause; exclude confounders (drugs, hypothermia < 36 °C, metabolic, NM blockade); SBP ≥ 100; core ≥ 36 °C.
  2. Coma + absent brainstem reflexes: pupils fixed; no corneal/oculocephalic/caloric/gag/cough; no motor to noxious cranial nerve stim.
  3. Apnoea test: pre-oxygenate; disconnect; observe 8–10 min; ABG. Positive: no respiratory effort + PaCO₂ ≥ 60 (or ≥ 20 above baseline). Abort if SBP < 90, SpO₂ < 85%, arrhythmia.
  4. Ancillary: cerebral angiography (gold), EEG, nuclear flow, TCD.
  5. Spinal reflexes (Lazarus, triple flexion) can persist and do NOT preclude brain death.
9. Post-arrest HIE and multimodal prognostication (Modules 13, 22)
  • TTM 32–36 °C × 24 h (TTM2 2021: 33 vs 36 — normothermia 36 non-inferior).
  • AVOID hyperthermia post-arrest.
  • Multimodal prognostication after ≥ 72 h off sedation — combine ≥ 2 of:
    • Bilateral absent N20 SSEPs
    • Status myoclonus day 1
    • NSE > 60 ng/mL at 48–72 h
    • EEG burst-suppression or status without reactivity
    • MRI DWI cytotoxic oedema (cortical ribbon, basal ganglia)
  • NEVER prognosticate on a single test, especially < 72 h.
  • Lance-Adams (action myoclonus in awake survivors weeks later) — clonazepam, valproate.
  • Delayed post-hypoxic leukoencephalopathy 1–4 weeks later.
10. Disorders of consciousness (Module 25)
  • Use CRS-R to distinguish UWS, MCS−, MCS+, EMCS.
  • Up to 40% of "vegetative" patients reclassify as MCS on careful CRS-R; covert cognition demonstrated by fMRI/EEG (Owen, Monti).
  • Amantadine 200 → 400 mg/day (Giacino NEJM 2012) improves recovery in TBI 4–16 weeks post-injury.
  • Avoid absolute statements before 72 h prognostication window.

Case Vignettes

Case 1 — A 24-year-old found unresponsive at home with empty pill bottles. Pinpoint pupils. RR 6. GCS 4

Diagnosis: Opioid overdose.

Plan:

  1. A: bag-mask ventilation; B: pulse ox; C: IV access.
  2. Naloxone 0.04 mg IV titrated to respiratory rate (start low to avoid acute withdrawal in opioid-dependent); fentanyl needs higher doses + infusion.
  3. Finger-stick glucose; thiamine before glucose if any nutritional risk.
  4. ABG, paracetamol level (commonest co-ingestant), salicylate, lactate, ECG, troponin.
  5. Co-oximetry (CO).
  6. Tox screen; do not rely on negative urine screen.
  7. Admit to monitored bed for several hours after naloxone (half-life shorter than most opioids — re-sedation likely).
  8. Connect to addiction services + naloxone take-home + harm reduction (Module 13).
Case 2 — A 68-year-old in AF on warfarin. Acute coma. CT shows large left thalamic ICH with intraventricular extension. Right hemiparesis. INR 3.4

Diagnosis: Warfarin-related thalamic ICH with IVH and acute hydrocephalus.

Plan:

  1. A: intubate (GCS < 8); B: ventilation strategy with PaCO₂ 35–40; C: BP control to SBP 140 (INTERACT-2 / ATACH-2 framework).
  2. Anticoagulation reversal: 4F-PCC 25–50 IU/kg + vitamin K 10 mg IV slowly.
  3. Head elevation 30°; treat fever; mannitol 1 g/kg or hypertonic 3% saline 250 mL bolus for clinical herniation.
  4. Neurosurgery consult: external ventricular drain for IVH + hydrocephalus.
  5. ICH score for prognostication (GCS + age + ICH volume + IVH + infratentorial).
  6. Treat seizures; avoid prophylactic antiepileptics unless seizure occurred.
  7. Re-anticoagulation timing after weeks based on bleed control + clot burden.
  8. Cross-ref Modules 2, 22.
Case 3 — A 55-year-old with sudden coma. Quadriplegic with eyes open. Examination shows preserved vertical gaze and blinking. He blinks to commands

Diagnosis: Locked-in syndrome from basilar artery thrombosis (ventral pontine infarct).

Plan:

  1. URGENT non-contrast CT + CT angiography.
  2. If basilar occlusion: mechanical thrombectomy (basilar Class 1 per AHA/ASA 2026; wider window than anterior circulation).
  3. IV thrombolysis if eligible.
  4. Establish communication channel using vertical gaze and blinking (eye-gaze board → AAC).
  5. ICU + stroke unit + careful BP management.
  6. Family education: patient is AWARE — speak to them.
  7. Early rehabilitation; long-term DOC clinic if recovery prolonged (Module 25).
  8. Cross-ref Modules 2, 18.
Case 4 — A 35-year-old pregnant patient at 32 weeks. Sudden severe headache + seizure + coma. BP 200/130. Hyperreflexia. CT shows occipital vasogenic oedema

Diagnosis: Eclampsia with posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome (PRES).

Plan:

  1. A: intubate; B; C: cautious BP control to 140–160/90–100 (over-rapid reduction worsens cerebral ischaemia) — labetalol IV or hydralazine.
  2. Magnesium sulphate 4–6 g IV loading then 1–2 g/h infusion × 24 h post-delivery (Magpie trial — Module 20).
  3. Plan urgent delivery — definitive treatment.
  4. Continuous fetal monitoring.
  5. Avoid magnesium if known myasthenia gravis (use benzodiazepines + cautious BP).
  6. MRI post-stabilisation for PRES vs RCVS.
  7. Resolution typically within days–weeks of trigger removal.
  8. Cross-ref Modules 20, 22.
Case 5 — A 70-year-old man post out-of-hospital cardiac arrest. Day 4. Off sedation 36 h. Examiner asks whether to withdraw care. GCS 6, status myoclonus on day 1, NSE 85 ng/mL at 72 h, EEG burst-suppression without reactivity, MRI DWI cortical ribbon

Diagnosis: Severe hypoxic-ischaemic encephalopathy with multimodal poor prognosis.

Plan:

  1. Confirm sedation washout (off ≥ 72 h; consider drug levels if uncertain — fentanyl, midazolam half-lives prolonged in renal/hepatic).
  2. Confirm no confounders: temperature, U&E, glucose, ammonia, drug effect.
  3. Multimodal poor prognostic signs (4 of 5 present): status myoclonus day 1 + NSE > 60 + EEG burst-suppression without reactivity + DWI cortical ribbon. Bilateral absent N20 SSEPs adds confirmation.
  4. Combine ≥ 2 of: bilateral absent N20, status myoclonus day 1, NSE > 60, EEG burst-suppression / unreactive status, MRI DWI cytotoxic — never single test.
  5. Family meeting with palliative care; respect prior advance directive; values-based decision; bereavement support.
  6. Avoid prognostication < 72 h; avoid sole reliance on clinical exam.
  7. If proceeding with WLST, document criteria met; otherwise continue care with planned re-assessment.
  8. Cross-ref Modules 13, 22, 25.
Case 6 — A 45-year-old man with refractory psychosis is now mute, immobile, with waxy flexibility and posturing. CK 1200. Temp 38.2 °C. On haloperidol

Differential: catatonia vs neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS). Distinguishing features matter.

Plan:

  1. STOP the haloperidol immediately.
  2. Differentiate: NMS has lead-pipe rigidity, hyperthermia >38.5–40, autonomic instability, CK often > 1000, develops over days; catatonia has waxy flexibility, posturing, mutism, echolalia, may have lower temp + less rigidity.
  3. If catatonia suspected: lorazepam challenge 1–2 mg IV — often dramatic response within minutes confirms diagnosis.
  4. If NMS: aggressive cooling, IV fluids, dantrolene + bromocriptine/amantadine, ICU monitoring, treat rhabdomyolysis (Module 13).
  5. If catatonia confirmed: scheduled lorazepam (start ~ 6 mg/day divided); ECT for refractory; treat underlying psychiatric illness.
  6. Avoid antipsychotics during acute catatonia (risk of NMS); restart after resolution and consider clozapine if needed.
  7. Cross-ref Modules 13 (hyperthermia syndromes), 24.
