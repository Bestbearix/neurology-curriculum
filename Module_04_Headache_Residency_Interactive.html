Module 4 — Headache & Facial Pain

Interactive residency module · exclude danger → diagnose → treat → prevent. Reference: printable reference ↗

The through-line

▎Core loop
  • Exclude danger with SNOOP before assigning a primary-headache label.
  • Diagnose by ICHD-3 — count attacks, duration, associated symptoms.
  • Treat acutely early; prevent when frequent, long, disabling, or acute therapy is overused.
The CGRP era: gepants (ubrogepant/rimegepant) and ditans (lasmiditan) give acute relief without vasoconstriction; anti-CGRP mAbs (erenumab/fremanezumab/galcanezumab/eptinezumab) and PREEMPT botox transformed prevention.
Two can't-miss patterns: thunderclap headache (SAH/RCVS/CVST) and new headache >50 with jaw claudication/scalp tenderness (GCA — treat before biopsy).

Migraine: diagnosis & biology

▎Key points
  • Without aura: ≥5 attacks, 4–72 h, ≥2 pain features + ≥1 associated symptom.
  • Chronic migraine: ≥15 headache days/mo >3 mo, ≥8 migraine-feature days.
  • Aura = cortical spreading depression; pain = trigeminovascular + CGRP release.
DiagnosisThresholdFeatures
Without aura≥5 attacks, 4–72 h≥2 pain + ≥1 associated
With aura≥2 attacksReversible aura 5–60 min
Chronic≥15 HA days/mo >3 mo≥8 migraine-feature days
Risk marker: episodic migraine associates with elevated Framingham cardiovascular risk (AMPP) — avoid triptans/ergots in uncontrolled vascular disease.

Acute treatment

▎Key points
  • Treat early, adequate dose; cap frequency to prevent MOH.
  • Gepants & ditans have no vasoconstriction — use when triptans contraindicated.
ClassExamplesNote
NSAIDs/analgesicsNaproxen, ibuprofen, ASAMild–moderate
TriptansSuma-, riza-, eletriptanAvoid in CAD/CVD, uncontrolled HTN

ACHIEVE: ubrogepant 50 mg beat placebo for 2-h pain freedom and most-bothersome-symptom freedom. Rimegepant works acutely and preventively.

Preventive treatment

▎Key points
  • Offer when ≈≥4 migraine days/mo with impairment, long/disabling, or acute overuse.
  • ≈50% reduction = success; give orals ~8 weeks at target dose.
  • Anti-CGRP mAbs work even after multiple failures; botox (PREEMPT) is chronic-only.

EHF 2022: recommends eptinezumab, erenumab, fremanezumab, galcanezumab for prevention. PREEMPT: 155 U / 31 sites q12 weeks for chronic migraine.

Cluster headache & the TACs

▎Key points
  • TACs share unilateral pain + ipsilateral autonomic features; they split on duration, frequency, and treatment response.
  • Cluster: 15–180 min, up to 8/day, circadian/seasonal bouts, agitation (pacing); high suicidality.
  • Cluster: acute O₂ + SC/nasal sumatriptan; bridge steroids/GON block; prevent verapamil (ECG!).
  • Identify the indomethacin-responsive TACs (paroxysmal hemicrania, hemicrania continua) — fully treatable.

Always trial indomethacin in a short-lasting unilateral autonomic headache — an absolute response reclassifies "atypical cluster" as paroxysmal hemicrania / hemicrania continua.

Tension-type & medication-overuse headache

▎Key points — TTH
  • Most prevalent primary headache (~26%): bilateral, pressing, non-pulsatile, mild–moderate, not worse with activity, no nausea.
  • Graded: infrequent episodic (<1/mo) · frequent episodic (1–14/mo) · chronic (≥15/mo).
  • Chronic TTH preventive of choice: amitriptyline (~100 mg ranked highest in network meta-analysis).
FeatureTTHMigraine
QualityBilateral, pressingUnilateral, pulsating
Severity/activityMild–mod, not aggravatedMod–severe, worsened
AssociatedNone / ≤1 photo-/phonophobiaNausea + photo/phonophobia
Reassess the label: disabling, throbbing "chronic TTH" with nausea/photophobia is often chronic migraine misclassified — the treatments differ. Botox is not established for chronic TTH.
▎Key points — MOH
  • Headache ≥15 days/mo >3 mo with acute-drug overuse — a secondary headache that maintains chronification.
  • Thresholds: ≥10 days/mo triptans/ergots/opioids/combos; ≥15 days/mo simple analgesics.
  • Pillars: educate + withdraw overused drug + start a preventive; mAbs/botox work without mandatory detox.
  • Screen sleep: chronic insomnia (~60%) and RLS (~37%) are common in MOH and reversible with treatment.
Drug classOveruse thresholdRisk
Simple analgesics≥15 days/moLower
Triptans / ergots≥10 days/moModerate
Combination analgesics≥10 days/moHigh
Opioids / butalbital≥10 days/moHighest
Butalbital and opioids are the highest-risk agents — avoid for migraine and taper (don't stop abruptly) because of withdrawal risk. Bridge with an NSAID course, steroids, or GON block.

Other primary headaches

▎Key points
  • Diagnoses of exclusion — cough/exercise/sexual/thunderclap types require imaging to rule out secondary causes first.
  • Many are indomethacin-responsive (cough, exercise, sexual, stabbing) — a positive response supports the diagnosis.
  • Hypnic headache wakes older patients from sleep → bedtime caffeine/lithium.

The indomethacin test: absolute response defines paroxysmal hemicrania & hemicrania continua, and is typical for primary cough/exercise/sexual/stabbing headache. Co-prescribe gastroprotection.

Secondary headaches

▎Key points
  • A SNOOP flag points here; vascular causes cluster around thunderclap (SAH, RCVS, CVST, dissection, apoplexy).
  • GCA is the vision-threatening >50 emergency — treat on suspicion.
  • ICP problems are positional: high ICP (IIH) worse supine; low CSF (SIH) worse upright.
  • Cervicogenic headache: side-locked pain from the upper cervical spine, confirmed by diagnostic block.

Don't miss: thunderclap → image + tap; new HA >50 with systemic/visual features → GCA; strictly orthostatic HA → SIH (treatable, often mislabeled chronic migraine).

Painful cranial neuropathies & neuralgias

▎Key points
  • Trigeminal neuralgia: electric-shock trigger-evoked facial pain; carbamazepine/oxcarbazepine first-line; MRI to separate classical vs secondary.
  • Microvascular decompression = most durable surgery when drugs fail (also preferred surgery for GPN).
  • Occipital neuralgia (diagnostic blocks) and Tolosa-Hunt (steroid-responsive) complete the differential.

Who was included & excluded

▎Why it matters
  • Acute gepant trials enrolled episodic migraine and excluded chronic migraine/MOH.
  • Prevention trials split by episodic vs chronic and by prior-failure count — match to your patient.
Ubrogepant — ACHIEVE (acute)
Included: adults 18–75 y, migraine onset <50 y, attacks 4–72 h; treat one moderate–severe attack. Randomized 1:1:1 placebo / 25 / 50 mg.
Excluded: difficulty distinguishing migraine from other headache; current chronic migraine; significant CV disease.
Endpoints: 2-h pain freedom + most-bothersome-symptom freedom.
Fremanezumab — episodic migraine
Included: adults 18–70 y, episodic migraine (6–14 headache days, ≥4 migraine days in 28-day baseline). Monthly vs quarterly vs placebo.
Endpoint: change in monthly migraine days over 12 weeks.
Fremanezumab — chronic migraine
Included: adults with chronic migraine (≥15 headache days/mo; baseline ~13 headache days). Quarterly vs monthly vs placebo SC.
Result: ~38–41% ≥50% responders vs 18% placebo.
FOCUS (fremanezumab, difficult-to-treat)
Included: adults 18–70 y, episodic OR chronic migraine, documented failure of 2–4 preventive classes in past 10 y.
Excluded: >4 class failures. Phase 3b, double-blind, placebo-controlled.
LIBERTY (erenumab) & galcanezumab (EM)
LIBERTY: adults 18–65 y, episodic migraine, 2–4 prior preventive failures; higher ≥50% response at weeks 9–12.
Galcanezumab EM: 120/240 mg vs placebo over 6 mo → ~4.2–4.3 fewer monthly migraine days.
OnabotulinumtoxinA — PREEMPT (chronic)
Included: adults with chronic migraine (≥15 headache days/mo). 155 U / 31 fixed sites q12 weeks.
Excluded: episodic migraine (no benefit) — botox is a chronic-migraine therapy only.
