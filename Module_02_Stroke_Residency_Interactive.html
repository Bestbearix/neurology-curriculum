Why this is the resident-depth template

The general-physician Module 2 teaches recognition and when-to-refer. This version assumes you are the stroke service: you localize, decide reperfusion, run the code, choose secondary prevention, and defend it on rounds. Every section adds mechanism, the trial that changed practice, and the number you must quote.

By the end you should be able to:
  1. Localize an ischemic syndrome to a specific vascular territory and name the classic deficit.
  2. Apply the TOAST classification and tailor the workup to mechanism.
  3. Quote the eligibility windows and exclusions for IV thrombolysis and mechanical thrombectomy, and the pivotal trials behind each.
  4. Manage blood pressure, glucose, and complications differently for ischemic vs hemorrhagic stroke.
  5. Select evidence-based secondary prevention by mechanism (LAA, cardioembolic, small-vessel, ESUS).
  6. Recognize and triage SAH and ICH, including anticoagulation reversal.
Time is brain. ~1.9 million neurons are lost per minute in a typical untreated large-vessel ischemic stroke. Every workflow target — door-to-needle <60 min, door-to-puncture <90 min — exists to shrink that number.
Educational use only. Management figures reflect widely established guidelines and landmark trials as a teaching reference; always follow current institutional protocols and the latest guideline at the point of care.

The five classic lacunar syndromes

Small deep infarcts (<15 mm) from lipohyalinosis of penetrating arteries. No cortical signs (no aphasia, neglect, visual field cut).

  • Pure motor — posterior limb of internal capsule / basis pontis (most common)
  • Pure sensory — VPL thalamus
  • Sensorimotor — thalamocapsular
  • Ataxic hemiparesis — pons / internal capsule
  • Dysarthria–clumsy hand — pons / genu of internal capsule
Cortical sign present? It is not a lacune — look for a large-vessel or embolic source.

Ischemic core vs penumbra — the physiology you defend on rounds

Cerebral blood flow normally ~50 mL/100g/min. Below ~20, neurons are electrically silent but viable — the penumbra (salvageable). Below ~10, the core infarcts irreversibly. Reperfusion therapy exists to save penumbra before it becomes core; collaterals determine how fast that conversion happens, which is why "tissue clock" (perfusion mismatch) can extend the "time clock" in selected patients.

Young / cryptogenic stroke — broaden the net
  • Cervical artery dissection — neck pain/Horner, recent trauma/chiropractic; vessel-wall imaging
  • PFO with paradoxical embolism — bubble study; RoPE score; closure in selected <60y
  • Hypercoagulable — antiphospholipid syndrome, malignancy
  • RCVS / vasculitis — thunderclap headache, "string-of-beads"
  • Genetic — CADASIL, Fabry, MELAS, sickle cell

The trial evidence base

Know these by name, window, and the practice change they produced — this is the high-yield core of stroke for RITE and oral boards.

IV thrombolysis

NINDS (NEJM 1995) — IV alteplase 0.9 mg/kg ≤3 h: global OR for favorable outcome 1.7 (1.2–2.6); symptomatic ICH 6.4% vs 0.6%, yet net benefit favors treatment. Established thrombolysis.
ECASS III (NEJM 2008) — alteplase 3–4.5 h improved 90-day mRS 0–1 in selected patients (original exclusions: age >80, NIHSS >25, anticoagulation, prior stroke+diabetes).
Tenecteplase — higher fibrin specificity, longer half-life, single bolus; at 0.25 mg/kg ≥comparable reperfusion vs alteplase, increasingly preferred before thrombectomy.
Wake-up / unknown onset — per ESO IVT guideline, MRI DWI–FLAIR mismatch selects patients last-seen-well >4.5 h who still benefit.

Mechanical thrombectomy

HERMES (Lancet 2016, IPD meta-analysis) — MR CLEAN, ESCAPE, SWIFT PRIME, EXTEND-IA, REVASCAT: anterior LVO ≤6 h, adjusted cOR 2.49 (1.76–3.53), NNT 2.6 for ≥1 mRS-level improvement; consistent across subgroups.
DAWN — thrombectomy 6–24 h by clinical–core mismatch. DEFUSE-3 — 6–16 h by perfusion–core mismatch. Pooled late-window 90-day independence favors thrombectomy.
ESO/ESMINT guideline — recommend thrombectomy + best medical care for anterior LVO ≤6 h; extend to 6–24 h with mismatch imaging.

Hemorrhagic stroke

INTERACT2 / ATACH-2 — target SBP ~140 is safe; below ~120 adds no benefit and risks renal events. Trajectory (pooled analysis): smooth, sustained 120–140 is best; large drops within 1 h reverse benefit — lower steadily, not abruptly.
Reversal & hematoma expansion — idarucizumab (dabigatran), andexanet alfa/4-factor PCC (Xa inhibitors), vitamin K + PCC (warfarin); TXA may modestly reduce expansion; watch the CT "spot sign."
Fever — INTREPID RCT — prophylactic normothermia (37 °C) cut fever burden but did not improve function; treat fever and find the cause, but routine aggressive cooling isn't outcome-improving.
Secondary-prevention landmark trials (preview)
  • CHANCE / POINT — short-course dual antiplatelet (aspirin + clopidogrel ~21–90 days) after minor stroke/high-risk TIA reduces recurrence.
  • SAMMPRIS — for intracranial stenosis, aggressive medical therapy beat stenting.
  • NASCET / ECST — carotid endarterectomy benefits symptomatic stenosis 70–99% (and selected 50–69%).

Acute management algorithms

The hyperacute ischemic pathway

  1. Last known well + NIHSS + fingerstick glucose (rule out hypoglycemia mimic).
  2. Non-contrast CT to exclude hemorrhage; CTA head/neck for LVO; perfusion if late window.
  3. IV thrombolysis if eligible (≤4.5 h or MRI mismatch) — target door-to-needle <60 min.
  4. Thrombectomy if LVO and within window (≤24 h with mismatch) — target door-to-puncture <90 min.
  5. Admit to stroke unit; start the mechanism workup (TOAST).
BP rules differ by treatment: for thrombolysis, lower to <185/110 before and keep <180/105 after. If not reperfusing, permissive hypertension — only treat if >220/120 (or end-organ injury).

Thrombolysis quick eligibility

IncludeKey exclusions
Disabling deficit, onset ≤4.5 h (or MRI mismatch), age ≥18 ICH on CT; recent intracranial surgery/major head trauma (3 mo); active bleeding; BP >185/110 unresponsive; platelets <100k; INR >1.7; DOAC within 48 h (normal renal); glucose-corrected mimic

Hemorrhagic stroke (ICH) bundle

  • Stop the bleed: reverse anticoagulation immediately (agent-specific).
  • BP: lower toward SBP ~140 (INTERACT2); avoid drops below ~120.
  • Treat raised ICP; neurosurgery for cerebellar hemorrhage >3 cm or deteriorating, hydrocephalus (EVD).
  • Find the cause: hypertensive (deep) vs amyloid (lobar, elderly) vs vascular malformation (young, CTA/MRA).

Subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH)

  • Thunderclap headache → non-contrast CT; if negative & high suspicion, LP for xanthochromia.
  • Secure aneurysm (coil/clip); nimodipine for vasospasm prophylaxis (improves outcome, not vasospasm rate per se).
  • Monitor for vasospasm (days 4–14), hydrocephalus, hyponatremia (cerebral salt wasting).

Universal measures, every ischemic stroke: high-intensity statin (LDL target low), BP control, diabetes/smoking management, and a documented TOAST mechanism that justifies the chosen antithrombotic.
AF + recent stroke timing: larger infarcts carry higher hemorrhagic-transformation risk, so anticoagulation is generally delayed longer in big strokes and started sooner in TIAs/small infarcts — individualize.
Minor stroke / high-risk TIA (CHANCE + POINT pooled): short-course clopidogrel–aspirin — benefit concentrated in the first ~21 days, bleeding accrues later → short course then single agent.
AF + atherosclerosis / stable CAD: adding antiplatelet to anticoagulation did not reduce events (17.8% vs 19.6%) and raised bleeding; edoxaban monotherapy beat dual therapy → anticoagulant monotherapy preferred.
PFO & cryptogenic stroke: ~50% of cryptogenic strokes have a PFO; with good selection (young, large shunt/atrial septal aneurysm) transcatheter closure reduces recurrence vs medical therapy.

Flashcards — tap to flip

Case-based reasoning

Case 1. 72-year-old, last known well 5 hours ago, sudden right hemiplegia and global aphasia. NIHSS 18. CT: no hemorrhage, hyperdense left MCA. CTA: left M1 occlusion. CT perfusion: core 15 mL, penumbra 90 mL.
What do you do — and why?

Outside the 4.5 h thrombolysis window, but a large perfusion–core mismatch with an M1 LVO in the 6–16 h window = mechanical thrombectomy (DEFUSE-3 / DAWN logic). Target door-to-puncture <90 min. Keep BP <185/110 peri-procedure; admit to stroke unit; begin TOAST workup (telemetry/echo — embolic-appearing, sudden maximal deficit suggests cardioembolism).

Case 2. 60-year-old with vertigo, vomiting, hoarseness, left facial numbness and right-body numbness, left Horner, ipsilateral limb ataxia.
Localize and name it.

Lateral medullary (Wallenberg) syndrome — PICA / vertebral territory. Crossed sensory pattern (ipsilateral face, contralateral body) localizes to the brainstem. Watch the airway/swallow (aspiration risk); image the posterior circulation and consider vertebral dissection in a younger patient with neck pain.

Case 3. 55-year-old on apixaban for AF presents with sudden left hemiparesis and decreased consciousness. CT: large right basal ganglia hemorrhage.
First moves?

Acute ICH on a factor Xa inhibitor: reverse immediately — andexanet alfa (or 4-factor PCC if unavailable). Lower SBP toward ~140 (INTERACT2). Neurosurgery consult; manage ICP; reverse, image vessels for an underlying lesion later. Deep location + hypertension history favors hypertensive etiology.
