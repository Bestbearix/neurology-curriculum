You are the stroke service

The general-physician Module 2 teaches recognition and when-to-refer. This enriched version assumes you localize, decide reperfusion, run the code, and choose secondary prevention — quoting the trial that changed practice and the number behind it.

By the end you should be able to:
  1. Localize an ischemic syndrome to a vascular territory and name the classic deficit.
  2. Apply TOAST and tailor the workup to mechanism.
  3. Quote thrombolysis/thrombectomy windows and pivotal trials.
  4. Manage BP, glucose and complications differently for ischemic vs hemorrhagic stroke.
  5. Select secondary prevention by mechanism (LAA, cardioembolic, small-vessel, ESUS).
  6. Triage SAH and ICH, including anticoagulation reversal.
Time is brain. ~1.9 million neurons lost per minute in untreated LVO stroke. Door-to-needle <60 min, door-to-puncture <90 min.

Vascular territories & syndromes

Key points
  • The deficit names the territory; the territory predicts mechanism + workup.
  • Anterior: MCA (face/arm > leg, aphasia/neglect), ACA (leg > arm), lacunes (pure motor, no cortical signs).
  • Posterior: PCA (hemianopia), brainstem crossed syndromes, cerebellar (ataxia — watch for swelling).
  • Cortical sign present → not a lacune.

The five lacunar syndromes (no cortical signs): pure motor, pure sensory, sensorimotor, ataxic hemiparesis, dysarthria–clumsy hand.
Cerebellar stroke trap: a "dizzy, vomiting" patient may swell over 24–72 h and herniate — low threshold for posterior imaging, admission, neurosurgical awareness.

Mechanism & TOAST

Key points
  • Every ischemic stroke earns a mechanism — it drives workup and prevention.
  • Five categories: large-artery atherosclerosis, cardioembolism, small-vessel, other determined, undetermined/ESUS.
  • Target the workup to the suspected mechanism, not a blanket panel.

Core vs penumbra — defend it on rounds

CBF ~50 mL/100g/min normally; <20 = penumbra (silent, salvageable); <10 = core (infarcted). Collaterals set the conversion rate — the basis for late-window, imaging-selected treatment.

Young / cryptogenic — broaden the net
  • Cervical dissection (neck pain, Horner)
  • PFO / paradoxical embolism (RoPE score)
  • Antiphospholipid, malignancy; RCVS; vasculitis
  • Genetic: CADASIL, Fabry, MELAS, sickle cell

The trial evidence base

Key points
  • Thrombolysis: ≤4.5 h (NINDS OR 1.7; ECASS III 3–4.5 h); tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg single bolus; MRI mismatch for wake-up.
  • Thrombectomy: HERMES cOR 2.49, NNT 2.6 (≤6 h); DEFUSE-3 6–16 h & DAWN 6–24 h by mismatch.
  • Know each trial by name, window, and the number it changed.

IV thrombolysis

NINDS (1995) — alteplase 0.9 mg/kg ≤3 h: OR 1.7 (1.2–2.6); symptomatic ICH 6.4% vs 0.6%; net benefit favors treatment.
ECASS III (2008) — extended to 3–4.5 h (exclusions: age >80, NIHSS >25, anticoagulation, prior stroke+diabetes).
Tenecteplase — single bolus, ≥comparable reperfusion; increasingly preferred pre-thrombectomy.
Wake-up (ESO): MRI DWI–FLAIR mismatch selects unknown-onset patients.

Mechanical thrombectomy

HERMES (2016) — anterior LVO ≤6 h: adjusted cOR 2.49 (1.76–3.53), NNT 2.6. Practice-defining.
DEFUSE-3 6–16 h (perfusion mismatch); DAWN 6–24 h (clinical–core mismatch).

Hemorrhagic

INTERACT2 / ATACH-2 — target ~SBP 140; below ~120 no benefit + renal harm. Trajectory: smooth sustained 120–140.
Reversal — idarucizumab (dabigatran); andexanet/4-factor PCC (Xa inhibitors); vitamin K + PCC (warfarin).

Who was included / excluded — tap to expand

The eligibility criteria are the indication. These are the original trial populations.

NINDS (alteplase 0–3 h)

Included: ischemic stroke, clearly defined onset ≤3 h, measurable deficit, CT with no hemorrhage, age ≥18.

Excluded: stroke/serious head trauma in prior 3 mo; major surgery ≤14 days; any prior intracranial hemorrhage; rapidly improving or minor deficit; seizure at onset; BP >185/110; anticoagulant/heparin with abnormal coagulation (PT >15 s, platelets <100k); glucose <50 or >400 mg/dL.

ECASS III (alteplase 3–4.5 h)

Included: acute ischemic stroke, onset 3–4.5 h, age 18–80, no hemorrhage or >⅓ MCA early infarct.

Extra exclusions (vs 0–3 h): age >80; NIHSS >25; oral anticoagulant use (any INR); combination of prior stroke and diabetes. (Later guidelines relaxed these.)

HERMES (thrombectomy ≤6 h)

Included: proximal anterior-circulation LVO (intracranial ICA / M1, some M2), treatable ~≤6 h, good pre-stroke function, favorable early imaging (components often required ASPECTS ≥6); most also got IV alteplase.

Excluded: large established core (low ASPECTS); posterior/distal occlusions; significant pre-stroke disability. Benefit held across age (incl. ≥80), sex, severity, site.

DAWN (6–24 h) & DEFUSE-3 (6–16 h)

DAWN — included: anterior LVO 6–24 h with clinical–core mismatch (age-banded): ≥80 y → NIHSS ≥10 & core <21 mL; <80 y → NIHSS ≥10 & core <31 mL, or NIHSS ≥20 & core 31–<51 mL.

DEFUSE-3 — included: anterior LVO 6–16 h with perfusion–core mismatch: core <70 mL, mismatch ratio ≥1.8, mismatch volume ≥15 mL.

Excluded (both): core too large for the band; insufficient mismatch; pre-stroke mRS >1.

CHANCE + POINT (short-course DAPT)

Included: minor ischemic stroke (NIHSS ≤3) or high-risk TIA (ABCD² ≥4), randomized within 24 h (CHANCE) or 12 h (POINT).

Excluded: disabling stroke/higher NIHSS; cardioembolic source needing anticoagulation; planned thrombolysis/thrombectomy. Benefit concentrated in the first ~21 days.

AF + stable CAD (edoxaban monotherapy)

Included: atrial fibrillation (anticoagulation indicated) + stable coronary disease (prior revascularization or medically managed, stable ≥6–12 mo).

Excluded / comparison: recent ACS/PCI (not stable); compared edoxaban monotherapy vs edoxaban + antiplatelet → monotherapy preferred.

INTERACT2 / ATACH-2 (acute ICH BP)

Included: spontaneous ICH within ~4.5–6 h, systolic BP 150–220 mm Hg.

Excluded: structural cause (aneurysm/AVM/tumour), planned early surgery, very large hematoma/imminent herniation. Best = smooth sustained SBP 120–140; targeting <120 added no benefit + more renal events.

PFO closure (cryptogenic stroke)

Included: younger patients (~18–60 y) with a cryptogenic embolic stroke after thorough workup; PFO confirmed, ideally high-risk (large shunt / atrial septal aneurysm).

Excluded: another identified mechanism (AF, large-artery, small-vessel); older patients / small low-risk PFO.

Acute management

Key points
  • Sequence: LKW + NIHSS + glucose → CT (blood) + CTA (LVO) ± perfusion → thrombolysis → thrombectomy.
  • BP depends on treatment: lysing → <185/110 before, <180/105 after; not reperfusing → treat only >220/120.
  • Door-to-needle <60 min; door-to-puncture <90 min; admit to a stroke unit.
  1. Last known well + NIHSS + fingerstick glucose (rule out hypoglycemia).
  2. Non-contrast CT (hemorrhage) + CTA head/neck (LVO); perfusion if late window.
  3. IV thrombolysis if eligible (≤4.5 h or MRI mismatch).
  4. Thrombectomy if LVO within window (≤24 h with mismatch).
  5. Stroke unit; begin TOAST workup.
BP rules differ: thrombolysis → <185/110 before, <180/105 for 24 h. Not reperfusing → permissive; treat only if >220/120 (abrupt lowering extends the infarct).
Fever — INTREPID: prophylactic normothermia cut fever burden but did NOT improve outcomes — treat fever & find the cause, don't rely on routine cooling.

Intracerebral hemorrhage bundle

  • Reverse anticoagulation immediately (agent-specific).
  • Lower BP smoothly toward SBP ~140 (INTERACT2).
  • Neurosurgery: cerebellar >3 cm or deteriorating, hydrocephalus (EVD).
  • Etiology: deep = hypertensive; lobar (elderly) = amyloid; young → vascular lesion.

Subarachnoid hemorrhage

  • Thunderclap → CT; if negative & suspicious → LP for xanthochromia.
  • Secure aneurysm (coil/clip); nimodipine; monitor vasospasm (days 4–14), hydrocephalus, hyponatremia.

Secondary prevention by mechanism

Key points
  • Match antithrombotic to mechanism; layer universal measures (statin, BP, glucose) on top.
  • Minor stroke/high-risk TIA: short DAPT ~21 days then single (CHANCE/POINT).
  • AF + atherosclerosis/stable CAD: anticoagulant monotherapy (adding antiplatelet ↑bleeding, no benefit).
  • Intracranial stenosis: medical > stenting (SAMMPRIS). ESUS: antiplatelet, not anticoagulation.

CHANCE + POINT: short clopidogrel–aspirin (~21 days) after minor stroke/high-risk TIA reduces recurrence; bleeding accrues later → short course then single.
AF + atherosclerosis / stable CAD: adding antiplatelet to anticoagulation didn't reduce events (17.8% vs 19.6%) & raised bleeding; edoxaban monotherapy preferred.
PFO: ~50% of cryptogenic strokes have a PFO; with good selection (young, large shunt/ASA) closure reduces recurrence.
AF + recent stroke timing: delay anticoagulation longer in large infarcts, sooner after TIA/small infarct ("1–3–6–12-day" heuristic), individualized.

Flashcards — tap to flip

Case-based reasoning

Case 1. 72-year-old, last known well 5 h ago, right hemiplegia + global aphasia, NIHSS 18. CT: no blood, hyperdense left MCA. CTA: left M1 occlusion. Perfusion: core 15 mL, penumbra 90 mL.
What do you do?

Outside the 4.5 h lysis window, but a large perfusion–core mismatch with M1 LVO in the 6–16 h window = mechanical thrombectomy (DEFUSE-3/DAWN). Door-to-puncture <90 min; BP <185/110 peri-procedure; embolic-appearing → telemetry/echo for cardioembolism.

Case 2. 60-year-old with vertigo, vomiting, hoarseness, left facial numbness + right-body numbness, left Horner, ipsilateral ataxia.
Localize and name it.

Lateral medullary (Wallenberg) syndrome — PICA/vertebral. Crossed sensory pattern = brainstem. Watch airway/swallow; consider vertebral dissection in a younger patient.

Case 3. 55-year-old on apixaban for AF, sudden left hemiparesis + drowsiness; CT: large right basal ganglia hemorrhage.
First moves?

ICH on a factor Xa inhibitor → reverse immediately (andexanet alfa or 4-factor PCC). Lower SBP smoothly toward ~140 (INTERACT2). Neurosurgery consult; manage ICP. Deep location + HTN favors hypertensive etiology.
