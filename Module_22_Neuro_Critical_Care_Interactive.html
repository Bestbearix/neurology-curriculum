Why this module?

Neuro-critical care is where high-stakes neurology and intensive-care medicine meet. The general physician's job is to recognise the time-critical emergencies, take the first protective actions, and route the patient through tiered management or escalate to neurosurgery and neuro-ICU. Every minute matters in raised ICP, status epilepticus, malignant MCA infarct, cerebellar herniation, SAH vasospasm, and brain death determination.

After this module you will:

  • Recognise impending herniation and initiate the first protective steps within minutes.
  • Apply the modern tiered raised-ICP algorithm (SIBICC 2019).
  • Manage status epilepticus through phases per ESETT 2019 evidence (LEV / fosphenytoin / VPA all ~50% efficacy).
  • Identify candidates for decompressive hemicraniectomy (DESTINY/HAMLET/DECIMAL pooled meta-analysis; DESTINY II for > 60).
  • Apply early intensive BP control (SBP < 140) for spontaneous ICH (INTERACT2/ATACH-2/INTERACT-3).
  • Run post-cardiac-arrest care with targeted normothermia (TTM2) and multimodal neuroprognostication ≥ 72 h.
  • Conduct brain death determination per WBDP/BDDD 2023 / AAN 2010, including prerequisites + clinical exam + apnoea test + ancillary if needed.
  • Triage neuromuscular crises (GBS, MG) and recognise ICU-acquired weakness.
The single principle: nothing inside the skull rescues a brain that has not been kept oxygenated, perfused, normoglycaemic, normocapnic, normothermic, normonatraemic, and seizure-free. Treat the body to save the brain.

2. Monro-Kellie + CPP physiology
  • Fixed box: brain (80%) + blood (10%) + CSF (10%). Any one ↑ → others must ↓ or ICP rises.
  • Normal ICP 7–15 mmHg. CPP = MAP − ICP; target 60–70 mmHg.
  • Autoregulation often impaired after injury → pressure-passive brain. Small drops in MAP cause ischaemia; surges cause oedema.
  • PaCO₂ is the strongest CBF modulator; hyperventilation only as brief rescue for impending herniation.
Cushing's triad is a LATE sign. Act earlier on pupil asymmetry, deteriorating GCS, posturing.
3. Tiered raised-ICP management (SIBICC 2019 / BTF 4e)

Tier 0 (all): HOB 30°, head midline, loosen collar; sedation + analgesia; SpO₂ 94%+, PaCO₂ 35–40; normothermia; glucose 6–10; Na 140–145; treat seizures.

Tier 1 (ICP > 22): bolus sedation; EVD insertion (monitors + drains CSF); hyperosmolar — 3% saline 250 mL over 15 min OR mannitol 0.5–1 g/kg over 15 min; HTS preferred if haemodynamically labile.

Tier 2: deeper sedation; neuromuscular blockade (cisatracurium); mild hyperventilation PaCO₂ 32–35 (with PbtO₂ monitoring if available); re-bolus hyperosmolar; consider decompressive craniectomy.

Tier 3: decompressive craniectomy; barbiturate coma (pentobarbital → burst-suppression on EEG); therapeutic hypothermia rarely used (Eurotherm 2015 no benefit and possibly harm in TBI).

Brief hyperventilation to PaCO₂ ~30 is a bridge to definitive treatment for impending herniation. Never sustained — vasoconstriction worsens ischaemia.

6. Malignant MCA infarct + decompressive craniectomy
  • Mortality up to 80% without surgery.
  • DESTINY/DECIMAL/HAMLET pooled (Vahedi Lancet Neurol 2007): hemicraniectomy within 48 h in patients < 60 reduces mortality by ~50% and increases mRS ≤ 3.
  • DESTINY II (Jüttler NEJM 2014): > 60 → reduces mortality but more severe disability; mRS 4 most common outcome — shared decision-making.
  • Indications: NIHSS > 15 (R) or > 20 (L), large MCA territory, declining GCS, midline shift, < 48 h.
  • Cerebellar emergencies: EVD for hydrocephalus + suboccipital decompression for cerebellar haemorrhage > 3 cm or with brainstem compression/hydrocephalus (AHA/ASA ICH 2022).
7. SAH + vasospasm + DCI
  • Thunderclap headache + meningism. CT > 99% sensitive < 6 h; LP for xanthochromia after 12 h if CT normal.
  • Secure aneurysm (coil or clip) within 72 h.
  • Pre-securing: SBP < 140; avoid hypotension.
  • Nimodipine 60 mg po q4h × 21 days — reduces DCI poor outcomes. Magnesium has not shown benefit (MASH II).
  • EVD for hydrocephalus (~30%).
  • DCI days 4–14: clinical assessment + TCD/CTA/CTP surveillance.
  • Treatment of new deficit: induced hypertension (SBP 160–200) + euvolaemia (NOT hypervolaemia — harmful); endovascular angioplasty / vasodilators in refractory cases.
  • Hyponatraemia: CSW (volume depleted → saline + fludrocortisone) vs SIADH (euvolaemic → fluid restriction).
8. Spontaneous ICH — BP, reversal, surgery
  • BP target SBP < 140 within 1 h (INTERACT2 / ATACH-2 / INTERACT-3 2023 care bundle — favourable outcome benefit).
  • ICH score (Hemphill 2001) for risk stratification.
  • Reverse anticoagulation:
    • Warfarin: 4F-PCC 25–50 IU/kg + vitamin K 10 mg IV.
    • Dabigatran: idarucizumab 5 g IV.
    • Apixaban/rivaroxaban: andexanet alfa (ANNEXA-I NEJM 2024) or 4F-PCC.
    • LMWH: protamine partial.
  • Surgery:
    • Cerebellar > 3 cm or with brainstem compression / hydrocephalus.
    • Selected lobar — ENRICH NEJM 2024: minimally invasive endoscopic surgery improved functional outcome.
    • Decompressive craniectomy for refractory mass effect.
  • Seizure prophylaxis not routine. VTE prophylaxis: mechanical from admission; pharmacological after 24–48 h if stable.
9. Post-cardiac-arrest care + neuroprognostication

TTM2 (Dankiewicz NEJM 2021): targeted normothermia (avoid fever ≤ 37.7 °C) equivalent to 33 °C in OHCA. Many guidelines now recommend aggressive fever avoidance ≤ 37.5 °C for ≥ 72 h; controlled hypothermia 32–36 °C remains an option.

SpO₂ 94–98% (avoid hyperoxia); PaCO₂ 35–40; MAP > 65 often 80–90; glucose 7.8–10; treat seizures and status myoclonus; continuous EEG.

Multimodal neuroprognostication ≥ 72 h after rewarming + sedation off (ERC/ESICM 2021)

  • Clinical: bilateral absent pupillary + corneal reflexes; status myoclonus day 1
  • SSEPs: bilateral absent N20
  • EEG: burst-suppression with identical bursts; suppressed background; GPDs > 50%; status epilepticus on flat background
  • Biomarker: NSE > 60 µg/L at 48–72 h
  • Imaging: diffuse cortical / BG / cerebellar cytotoxic oedema on MRI DWI; loss of grey-white differentiation on CT
Combine ≥ 2 robust features; do not prognosticate too early; sedation/hypothermia confound the exam. Lance-Adams (post-anoxic action myoclonus) is a RECOVERY state, not poor prognosis.
10. Brain death determination (WBDP/BDDD 2023; AAN 2010)

Prerequisites (all must be met):

  1. Established irreversible catastrophic brain injury of known cause.
  2. Exclude confounders: core temp ≥ 36 °C; SBP ≥ 100 (off vasoactive as feasible); normal metabolic state; CNS depressants + NMBs cleared (5 half-lives, levels, train-of-four); no mimicker (GBS, locked-in, fulminant Wernicke, toxin).
  3. Observation typically 6–24 h after the inciting event (longer for HIE).

Clinical examination — coma + absent brainstem reflexes + apnoea:

  1. Unresponsive coma (no eye opening, motor response, or vocalisation to noxious — supraorbital, nailbed).
  2. Absent brainstem reflexes: pupillary (4–9 mm fixed); corneal; oculocephalic (only if C-spine cleared); oculovestibular (50 mL ice-cold water bilateral with head 30°); gag; cough; no facial movement to noxious.
  3. Apnoea test: pre-oxygenate 100% O₂ × 10 min; disconnect ventilator + insufflate O₂ 6 L/min; observe 8–10 min for respiration; ABG; positive if PaCO₂ ≥ 60 mmHg (or rises ≥ 20 above baseline) AND no respiratory effort. Abort for hypotension / hypoxia / arrhythmia.

Ancillary testing when apnoea test cannot be done or exam confounded: cerebral angiography (no intracranial flow at carotid bifurcation), EEG (electrocerebral silence ≥ 30 min), TCD (oscillating / reverberating flow), nuclear medicine HMPAO (hollow skull).

Spinal reflexes can PERSIST — triple flexion, Lazarus sign, DTRs, sweating, BP variability do NOT exclude brain death (spinal origin).
11. Multimodal monitoring + ICU practicalities
  • ICP monitor: EVD (drains + monitors) or intraparenchymal (Camino bolt).
  • PbtO₂ > 20 mmHg: BOOST-3 NEJM 2024 — PbtO₂-guided care in severe TBI improved functional outcomes vs ICP-only.
  • Continuous EEG: detect NCSE (10–30% of comatose ICU); titrate anaesthetic in super-refractory status.
  • TCD for SAH vasospasm; NIRS; pupillometer (NPi).
  • Sedation: propofol (PRIS risk > 4 mg/kg/h or > 48 h — metabolic acidosis, rhabdo, cardiac failure); midazolam (status); dexmedetomidine (delirium / weaning, watch bradycardia); ketamine (super-refractory status, raises BP).
  • NMB (cisatracurium): refractory ICP, shivering during TTM, severe dyssynchrony.
  • ICU-acquired weakness: critical illness PN + myopathy after sepsis/MOF; mitigate with early mobilisation + nutrition + glycaemic control (Module 9).
12. Neuromuscular crises in the ICU
  • GBS respiratory failure (Module 9): 20/30/40 rule (FVC < 20, NIF less negative than −30, MEP < 40); intubate. Avoid succinylcholine (hyperkalaemia from upregulated AChRs). IVIG 0.4 g/kg/day × 5 d or PLEX 5 sessions. Autonomic instability is major mortality driver; telemetry; avoid pushed boluses.
  • Myasthenic crisis (Module 9): avoid aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones, β-blockers, magnesium (Module 20). PLEX or IVIG; steroids carefully; pyridostigmine held in severe crisis. Watch cholinergic crisis from pyridostigmine overuse.
  • High-cervical transverse myelitis (Module 10): respiratory failure possible.
  • Botulism, tetanus, ALS, periodic paralysis — Module 9.

Case Vignettes

Case 1 — A 28-year-old man with severe TBI (GCS 5, fixed dilated right pupil) is intubated and has ICP 35 mmHg on initial monitor

CT: large right hemispheric contusion with midline shift; basal cisterns effaced.

Diagnosis: Severe TBI with impending uncal herniation and refractory ICP.

Plan within minutes:

  1. Tier 0: head of bed 30°, head midline, loosen ETT ties / collar, propofol + fentanyl sedation, SpO₂ ≥ 94%, PaCO₂ 35–40, normothermia, glucose 6–10, Na 140–145.
  2. Tier 1: bolus sedation; 3% hypertonic saline 250 mL over 15 min; insert EVD for ICP monitoring + CSF drainage.
  3. Tier 2: deeper sedation + neuromuscular blockade (cisatracurium); brief mild hyperventilation to PaCO₂ 32–35 with PbtO₂ monitoring if available.
  4. Tier 3: decompressive craniectomy if refractory (RESCUEicp NEJM 2016: improves mortality with high disability rate in survivors); barbiturate coma to burst-suppression as alternative.
  5. Brief hyperventilation to PaCO₂ ~30 mmHg as bridge to definitive treatment for impending herniation (do not sustain).
  6. Multimodal monitoring: ICP + PbtO₂ + continuous EEG.
  7. Family meeting + palliative care consultation early for shared decision-making about decompressive craniectomy (high mortality and disability without intervention; survives at cost of disability).
Case 2 — A 24-year-old has a generalised tonic-clonic seizure that has lasted 8 minutes. IV access obtained

Glucose 5.6 mmol/L. No history of epilepsy.

Diagnosis: Established convulsive status epilepticus.

Plan:

  1. ABCs; oxygen; check glucose (already done); thiamine before glucose if at risk (Module 13).
  2. First-line: lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg IV (max 4 mg) — typically 4 mg IV stat; repeat once if no response after 5 min.
  3. If IV unavailable: midazolam 10 mg IM (RAMPART NEJM 2012).
  4. If still in status after 2 doses → refractory status (20–40 min): levetiracetam 60 mg/kg IV (max 4500 mg) over 10 min (ESETT preferred for safety and pregnancy compatibility); fosphenytoin 20 mg PE/kg IV over 10 min and valproate 40 mg/kg IV over 10 min are equally effective per ESETT NEJM 2019.
  5. If still in status → super-refractory: ICU; intubate; midazolam infusion 0.05–0.5 mg/kg/h or propofol 30–250 µg/kg/min or pentobarbital; continuous EEG to titrate.
  6. Send: electrolytes (Na, Ca, Mg), ABG, ammonia, drug levels, toxicology, anti-NMDA-R + LGI1 + paraneoplastic panel if autoimmune encephalitis suspected (Module 11).
  7. Identify and treat underlying cause; rule out NCSE on EEG; consider pyridoxine 100 mg IV trial under EEG for refractory unexplained status (especially infants).
Case 3 — A 55-year-old man with a large left MCA infarct (NIHSS 22) is admitted at 12 hours from onset. He is becoming drowsier; midline shift 6 mm on follow-up CT at 24 h

No haemorrhagic transformation. Right hemiplegia + global aphasia.

Diagnosis: Malignant left MCA infarct — high mortality without surgery.

Plan:

  1. Urgent neurosurgical consultation for decompressive hemicraniectomy within 48 hours (DESTINY/DECIMAL/HAMLET pooled analysis: < 60 years → reduces mortality by ~50% and increases proportion with mRS ≤ 3).
  2. Counsel family about aphasia outcome — left-sided infarct in dominant hemisphere has more severe communication / vocational impact even with successful surgery.
  3. Pre-op: HOB 30°; mannitol or hypertonic saline if ICP signs; manage BP; monitor neurologically.
  4. Post-op: ICP / CPP management; meticulous fluid balance; planned cranioplasty at ~3 months.
  5. If > 60 years: DESTINY II (Jüttler NEJM 2014) — hemicraniectomy reduces mortality but most survivors have mRS 4 (moderately severe disability) — emphasise shared decision-making.
  6. Cross-ref Module 2.
Case 4 — A 60-year-old presents 1 hour after thunderclap headache with neck stiffness. Non-contrast CT shows diffuse SAH

CT angiography: 7 mm anterior communicating artery aneurysm. GCS 14. BP 175/95.

Diagnosis: Aneurysmal SAH (Hunt-Hess 2; Fisher 3 — high vasospasm risk).

Plan:

  1. Strict BP: SBP < 140 pre-securing (labetalol, nicardipine titration).
  2. Bed rest; stool softeners; analgesia; antiemetic.
  3. Urgent neurosurgery / interventional neuroradiology consultation: secure aneurysm within 72 hours (endovascular coiling or surgical clipping).
  4. Nimodipine 60 mg po q4h × 21 days from admission — reduces DCI poor outcomes.
  5. EVD if hydrocephalus develops (~30%).
  6. Post-securing: monitor for delayed cerebral ischaemia days 4–14 — daily clinical assessment + TCD / CTA / CTP.
  7. If new deficit / vasospasm: induced hypertension SBP 160–200 + euvolaemia (not hypervolaemia — harmful); endovascular angioplasty / vasodilators if refractory.
  8. Hyponatraemia surveillance: CSW vs SIADH.
  9. Cardiac complications: Takotsubo, neurogenic pulmonary oedema, troponin rise.
  10. Cross-ref Modules 4 + 19.
Case 5 — A 68-year-old on apixaban presents with a 25 mL right basal ganglia haemorrhage and GCS 12. BP 195/110

Spot sign present on CTA — high expansion risk.

Diagnosis: Spontaneous ICH on apixaban with active expansion risk.

Plan within the next hour:

  1. Reverse apixaban: andexanet alfa (ANNEXA-I NEJM 2024 — improved haemostatic outcome) or 4F-PCC 50 IU/kg if andexanet unavailable.
  2. BP control to SBP < 140 within 1 hour of presentation (INTERACT2 / ATACH-2 / INTERACT-3 2023 care bundle favourable outcome benefit) — nicardipine or labetalol IV infusion.
  3. Care bundle (INTERACT-3): BP control + glucose 6–10 + temperature ≤ 37.5 + reverse anticoagulation. Apply all.
  4. VTE prophylaxis: mechanical from admission; pharmacological after 24–48 h if bleeding stable.
  5. Seizure prophylaxis not routine; treat clinical or EEG-confirmed seizures.
  6. Surgical consult: ICH expansion / mass effect / deterioration; consider minimally invasive endoscopic surgery for lobar haemorrhage (ENRICH NEJM 2024); decompressive craniectomy for refractory mass effect; cerebellar > 3 cm requires suboccipital decompression.
  7. Restart anticoagulation: typically 2–8 weeks post-bleed depending on indication and bleeding cause; restart earlier if very high thrombotic risk (mechanical valve); cross-ref Module 2.
Case 6 — A 45-year-old man is unresponsive 6 days after a witnessed cardiac arrest (downtime 12 min, OHCA, VF arrest). He has been off sedation for 24 hours. Examination: no pupillary or corneal reflexes; no motor response to noxious; bilateral absent N20 on SSEPs; EEG shows burst-suppression with identical bursts; MRI DWI: diffuse cortical + basal-ganglia restricted diffusion

NSE day 3 was 110 µg/L.

Diagnosis: Severe hypoxic-ischaemic brain injury with very poor prognosis (≥ 2 robust features per ERC/ESICM 2021).

Plan:

  1. Multidisciplinary discussion including neurology, intensivist, palliative care, and family.
  2. Present neuroprognostication results with appropriate uncertainty; do not declare brain death unless WBDP/BDDD 2023 criteria are met (separate evaluation).
  3. Discuss values, goals of care, prior advance directives; consider withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment with palliative care support if futility consistent with family wishes.
  4. If proceeding to brain death evaluation: confirm prerequisites (temperature, BP, metabolic state, no confounders, sedation off ≥ 5 half-lives), conduct full clinical exam, apnoea test (if safe) or ancillary testing (cerebral angiography / nuclear medicine / EEG).
  5. If consistent with brain death: notify organ procurement organisation BEFORE withdrawal of support if donation considered.
  6. Document everything; family communication and bereavement support; consider chaplaincy / spiritual care.
  7. If decision is to continue support: optimise post-arrest care (target normothermia per TTM2; treat seizures; multidisciplinary rehab planning if any recovery emerges).
Avoid premature withdrawal in the first 72 hours. Lance-Adams (post-anoxic action myoclonus) is a RECOVERY state and does not equal poor prognosis.
