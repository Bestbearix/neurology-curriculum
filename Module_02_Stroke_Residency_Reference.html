1 Vascular anatomy & localizing syndromes

▎Key points
  • The deficit names the territory; the territory predicts the mechanism and the workup.
  • Anterior (carotid): MCA (face/arm > leg, aphasia/neglect), ACA (leg > arm), lenticulostriate lacunes (pure motor).
  • Posterior (vertebrobasilar): PCA (hemianopia), brainstem crossed syndromes, cerebellar (ataxia — watch for swelling).
  • Cortical sign present → not a lacune.

The five lacunar syndromes

Small deep infarcts (<15 mm) from lipohyalinosis of penetrating arteries, with no cortical signs: pure motor (internal capsule/pons), pure sensory (VPL thalamus), sensorimotor (thalamocapsular), ataxic hemiparesis, dysarthria–clumsy hand.

Cortical sign present? It is not a lacune — look for a large-vessel or embolic source.
Cerebellar stroke is a structural trap: a "dizzy, vomiting" patient may swell over 24–72 h, compress the brainstem, and hydrocephalus/herniate. Low threshold for posterior-circulation imaging, admission, and neurosurgical awareness (suboccipital decompression can be life-saving).

2 Mechanism & the TOAST classification

▎Key points
  • Every ischemic stroke earns a mechanism — it drives both workup and secondary prevention.
  • Five TOAST categories: large-artery atherosclerosis, cardioembolism, small-vessel, other determined, undetermined/ESUS.
  • Match the workup to the suspected mechanism, not a blanket panel.

3 Core, penumbra & the tissue clock

▎Key points
  • Normal cerebral blood flow ~50 mL/100 g/min. Below ~20 = penumbra (silent but salvageable); below ~10 = core (irreversible).
  • Collaterals determine how fast penumbra converts to core — the basis for late-window, imaging-selected treatment.

Reperfusion therapy exists to save penumbra before it becomes core. A favorable imaging profile (small core, large mismatch) can extend the "tissue clock" beyond the rigid "time clock" — which is why perfusion or clinical-mismatch selection opens the late window (§6).

4 Hyperacute ischemic management

▎Key points
  • Sequence: last-known-well + NIHSS + glucose → non-contrast CT (exclude blood) + CTA (LVO) ± perfusion → thrombolysis if eligible → thrombectomy if LVO.
  • BP depends on treatment: lysing → <185/110 before, <180/105 after; not reperfusing → permissive (treat only >220/120).
  • Stroke-unit care itself lowers mortality; start the TOAST workup on admission.
  1. Last known well, NIHSS, fingerstick glucose (exclude hypoglycemic mimic).
  2. Non-contrast CT (hemorrhage), CTA head/neck (LVO); perfusion if late window.
  3. IV thrombolysis if eligible (≤4.5 h or MRI mismatch) — door-to-needle <60 min.
  4. Thrombectomy if LVO within window (≤24 h with mismatch) — door-to-puncture <90 min.
  5. Admit to a stroke unit; begin the mechanism workup.
Blood pressure rules differ by treatment. Thrombolysis: lower to <185/110 before, keep <180/105 for 24 h. Not reperfusing: permissive hypertension — treat only if >220/120 (or end-organ injury), because abrupt lowering can extend the infarct.

Also attend to glucose, swallowing (NPO until a swallow screen passes), and — from the provided INTREPID trial — fever: prophylactic normothermia lowered fever burden but did not improve outcomes, so treat fever and find its cause rather than relying on routine aggressive cooling.

5 IV thrombolysis — evidence & eligibility

▎Key points
  • Standard window ≤4.5 h (NINDS 0–3 h; ECASS III 3–4.5 h); MRI DWI–FLAIR mismatch selects wake-up/unknown-onset patients.
  • Tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg (single bolus) is increasingly preferred, especially pre-thrombectomy.
  • Get BP <185/110 before treating; know the exclusions cold.
NINDS (NEJM 1995). IV alteplase 0.9 mg/kg within 3 h: global OR for a favorable outcome 1.7 (95% CI 1.2–2.6); symptomatic ICH 6.4% vs 0.6%, yet net benefit favors treatment. Established thrombolysis.
ECASS III (NEJM 2008). Alteplase 3–4.5 h improved 90-day disability (mRS 0–1); original extra exclusions: age >80, NIHSS >25, anticoagulation, prior stroke + diabetes.
Tenecteplase. Higher fibrin specificity, longer half-life, single bolus; ≥comparable reperfusion vs alteplase, increasingly preferred.
Wake-up / unknown onset. Per the ESO IVT guideline, MRI DWI–FLAIR mismatch selects patients last-seen-well >4.5 h who still benefit.

Disabling deficit, onset ≤4.5 h (or MRI mismatch), age ≥18ICH on CT; recent intracranial surgery/major head trauma (3 mo); active bleeding; BP >185/110 unresponsive; platelets <100k; INR >1.7; DOAC within ~48 h (normal renal); glucose-correctable mimic

6 Mechanical thrombectomy — evidence & windows

▎Key points
  • HERMES: anterior LVO ≤6 h — adjusted cOR 2.49, NNT 2.6 to reduce disability by ≥1 mRS level.
  • Late window: DEFUSE-3 (6–16 h, perfusion mismatch) and DAWN (6–24 h, clinical–core mismatch).
  • Candidacy: proximal anterior occlusion (ICA, M1, selected M2), salvageable imaging, reasonable baseline function.
HERMES (Lancet 2016, IPD meta-analysis). MR CLEAN, ESCAPE, SWIFT PRIME, EXTEND-IA, REVASCAT: adjusted common OR 2.49 (1.76–3.53; p<0.0001), NNT 2.6 — consistent across age, severity, occlusion site. One of the largest effects in vascular neurology.
Extended window — DAWN & DEFUSE-3. Perfusion- or clinical-core mismatch selects patients beyond 6 h; pooled 90-day functional-independence favors thrombectomy. DEFUSE-3: 6–16 h; DAWN: 6–24 h.

Guideline anchor (ESO/ESMINT): anterior-circulation LVO within 6 h → thrombectomy + best medical care; extend to 6–24 h with mismatch imaging. Thrombolysis, when indicated, is given alongside and must not delay transfer.

7 Intracerebral hemorrhage

▎Key points
  • Three priorities: stop the bleed (reverse anticoagulation immediately), control BP (smooth to ~SBP 140), find the cause.
  • Reversal: idarucizumab (dabigatran); andexanet/4-factor PCC (Xa inhibitors); vitamin K + PCC (warfarin).
  • Neurosurgery for cerebellar hemorrhage >3 cm or brainstem compression, hydrocephalus (EVD), selected accessible clots.
INTERACT2 / ATACH-2. Lowering to ~SBP 140 is safe and reasonable; the more aggressive target <120 (ATACH-2) added no benefit and caused more renal events.
BP trajectory (pooled INTERACT/ATACH-II). Moderate, smooth, sustained SBP 120–140 in the first hours is best; large rapid drops within 1 h (e.g., >200 to <140) diminish or reverse benefit — lower deliberately, not abruptly.
Against hematoma expansion: proven levers are early BP control and emergent anticoagulant reversal; TXA may modestly reduce expansion; watch for the CT "spot sign." Characterize etiology by location: deep = hypertensive; lobar (elderly) = amyloid; young = vascular malformation / venous sinus thrombosis (CTA/MRA/MRV).

8 Subarachnoid hemorrhage

▎Key points
  • Thunderclap headache → non-contrast CT; if negative & suspicious, LP for xanthochromia (CT sensitivity falls after ~6 h).
  • Secure the aneurysm early (coil/clip); give nimodipine (improves outcome via neuroprotection).
  • Monitor for vasospasm (days 4–14), hydrocephalus, seizures, hyponatremia (cerebral salt wasting).

9 Secondary prevention by mechanism

▎Key points
  • Match the antithrombotic to the mechanism; layer universal measures on top.
  • Minor stroke/high-risk TIA: short-course DAPT (~21 days) then single agent (CHANCE/POINT).
  • AF + atherosclerosis / stable CAD: anticoagulant monotherapy — adding antiplatelet doesn't help and raises bleeding.

Minor stroke / high-risk TIA — CHANCE + POINT. Clopidogrel–aspirin early after minor stroke/high-risk TIA reduces recurrence; benefit concentrated in the first ~21 days while bleeding accrues later → short course then single agent.
AF + atherosclerosis / stable CAD. Adding an antiplatelet to anticoagulation did not reduce the composite (17.8% vs 19.6%) and raised bleeding; edoxaban monotherapy beat dual therapy — anticoagulant monotherapy preferred.
PFO & cryptogenic stroke. ~50% of cryptogenic strokes have a PFO; with good selection (young, large shunt/atrial septal aneurysm) transcatheter closure reduces recurrence vs medical therapy.
Universal measures, every ischemic stroke: high-intensity statin (low LDL target), BP control, diabetes/smoking management, and a documented TOAST mechanism that justifies the antithrombotic.
AF + recent stroke timing: larger infarcts → higher hemorrhagic-transformation risk, so anticoagulation is delayed longer in big strokes and started sooner after TIA/small infarct (the "1–3–6–12-day" heuristic), individualized.

10 Special & young-stroke considerations

▎Key points
  • Under ~50, broaden the net: dissection, PFO, hypercoagulable, RCVS, vasculitis, genetic.
  • Cerebral venous sinus thrombosis is treated with anticoagulation even with hemorrhage.
  • Cervical artery dissection — neck/face pain, partial Horner, recent trauma/manipulation; vessel-wall imaging.
  • PFO / paradoxical embolism — bubble study, RoPE score; closure in selected <60 y.
  • Hypercoagulable — antiphospholipid syndrome, malignancy; RCVS (thunderclap + "string-of-beads"); CNS vasculitis.
  • Genetic — CADASIL, Fabry, MELAS, sickle cell.
  • CVST — headache, seizures, infarcts crossing arterial territories (often hemorrhagic); anticoagulate.

11 Landmark trials — who was included & excluded

▎Key points
  • Every practice-changing number came from a specific enrolled population — the eligibility criteria are the indication. Applying a result outside the trial population is off-label extrapolation.
  • The recurring gatekeepers: time window, imaging (hemorrhage/early-infarct/ASPECTS/mismatch), age, NIHSS severity, blood pressure, glucose, coagulation, and baseline function (pre-stroke mRS).
  • Modern guidelines have widened several of these (e.g., alteplase now used >80 y and NIHSS >25 in selected patients) — the columns below are the original trial criteria, which explain why the exclusions exist.

IV thrombolysis

NINDS (alteplase 0–3 h, NEJM 1995)

IncludedExcluded
  • Ischemic stroke with a clearly defined onset within 3 h and a measurable deficit
  • CT showing no intracranial hemorrhage
  • Age ≥18 (no upper limit)
  • Another stroke or serious head trauma in the prior 3 months; major surgery within 14 days
  • Any history of intracranial hemorrhage; symptoms suggesting SAH
  • Rapidly improving or minor symptoms; seizure at onset
  • GI or urinary hemorrhage within 21 days; arterial puncture at a non-compressible site
  • BP >185/110; anticoagulants/heparin with abnormal coagulation; PT >15 s, platelets <100,000
  • Glucose <50 or >400 mg/dL

Result: global OR 1.7 (1.2–2.6) for favorable 90-day outcome; symptomatic ICH 6.4% vs 0.6%.

ECASS III (alteplase 3–4.5 h, NEJM 2008)

IncludedExcluded (in addition to standard 0–3 h exclusions)
  • Acute ischemic stroke, onset 3–4.5 h before treatment
  • Age 18–80 years
  • No hemorrhage or large (>⅓ MCA) early infarct on imaging
  • Age >80 years
  • NIHSS >25 (severe stroke) or large infarct on imaging
  • Oral anticoagulant use (regardless of INR)
  • Combination of prior stroke AND diabetes

Result: favorable outcome (mRS 0–1) 52.4% vs 45.2%; more symptomatic ICH but no mortality difference. These four extra exclusions are historical; later guidelines relaxed them.

Tenecteplase

Randomized comparisons enrolled patients otherwise eligible for alteplase (within the standard time window, no hemorrhage), testing tenecteplase — most robustly at 0.25 mg/kg, and especially in the large-vessel-occlusion, pre-thrombectomy population. Exclusions mirror the alteplase criteria above.

Mechanical thrombectomy

HERMES pooled analysis (MR CLEAN, ESCAPE, SWIFT PRIME, EXTEND-IA, REVASCAT; Lancet 2016)

IncludedExcluded
  • Proximal anterior-circulation LVO (intracranial ICA or M1; some M2)
  • Thrombectomy achievable within ~6 h of onset (pooled up to 12 h in the analysis)
  • Most received IV alteplase where eligible; good pre-stroke function
  • Favorable/moderate early-ischemic imaging (component trials commonly required ASPECTS ≥6)
  • Large established infarct core (low ASPECTS) — varied by component trial
  • Posterior-circulation and distal occlusions
  • Poor baseline function / significant pre-stroke disability
  • Standard thrombolysis contraindications for those who also received alteplase

Result: adjusted common OR 2.49 (1.76–3.53); NNT 2.6 to reduce disability by ≥1 mRS level; benefit consistent across age (incl. ≥80), sex, severity, and occlusion site.

Late window — DAWN (6–24 h) & DEFUSE-3 (6–16 h)

Secondary prevention

CHANCE & POINT (short-course dual antiplatelet, pooled analysis)

IncludedExcluded
  • Minor ischemic stroke (NIHSS ≤3) or high-risk TIA (ABCD² ≥4)
  • Randomized to clopidogrel–aspirin vs aspirin within 24 h (CHANCE) or 12 h (POINT) of onset
  • Disabling stroke / higher NIHSS; cardioembolic source needing anticoagulation
  • Contraindication to antiplatelets; planned thrombolysis/thrombectomy

Result: fewer major ischemic events at 90 days (6.5% vs 9.1% pooled), with benefit concentrated in the first ~21 days (bleeding accrues thereafter) → short course then single agent.

AF + atherosclerosis / stable CAD (edoxaban monotherapy trial)

IncludedExcluded / comparison
  • Atrial fibrillation (anticoagulation indicated by CHA₂DS₂-VASc) plus stable coronary artery disease (prior revascularization or medically managed, ≥6–12 months stable)
  • Recent acute coronary syndrome / recent PCI (i.e., not stable)
  • Compared edoxaban monotherapy vs edoxaban + single antiplatelet

Result: monotherapy gave a better net outcome (fewer combined ischemic + bleeding events) → anticoagulant monotherapy preferred once CAD is stable.

Acute ICH blood pressure (INTERACT2 / ATACH-2 trajectory analysis)

IncludedExcluded
  • Spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage within ~4.5–6 h of onset
  • Elevated systolic BP 150–220 mm Hg
  • Structural cause (aneurysm, AVM, tumour), planned early surgery
  • Very large hematoma / imminent herniation; GCS very low (varied by trial)

Result: smooth, sustained lowering to SBP 120–140 is best; large abrupt drops within 1 h (e.g., >200 → <140) diminish or reverse benefit; targeting <120 (ATACH-2) added no benefit and more renal events.

PFO closure (cryptogenic stroke)

IncludedExcluded
  • Younger patients (broadly ~18–60 y) with a cryptogenic embolic stroke after thorough workup
  • PFO confirmed, ideally with high-risk features (large right-to-left shunt and/or atrial septal aneurysm)
  • Another identified stroke mechanism (AF, large-artery, small-vessel)
  • Older patients / small low-risk PFO where benefit is unproven

Result: with good patient selection, transcatheter closure reduces recurrent stroke versus antithrombotic therapy alone.

Reading criteria on rounds: when someone quotes a trial, ask "was this patient eligible?" A late-window thrombectomy candidate needs a qualifying mismatch; a thrombolysis candidate needs the time window and no exclusion; a PFO-closure candidate needs to be young with a truly cryptogenic stroke. The eligibility criteria are the indication.
