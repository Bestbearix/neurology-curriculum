Why this module?

Most disability after neurological disease results from how recovery is managed, not from the primary lesion. Stroke unit care + structured rehabilitation reduce dependency. Goal-directed multidisciplinary therapy transforms function and participation. The general physician's job is to know which interventions are evidence-based, when to escalate from outpatient to inpatient rehabilitation, when to involve specialist clinics (LSVT, spasticity, SCI), and how to set realistic patient-centred goals at the ICF activity and participation levels.

After this module you will:

  • Use the ICF framework to set goals at activity and participation levels.
  • Apply Kleim-Jones principles of experience-dependent plasticity.
  • Choose appropriate outcome measures (mRS, FIM, mBI, ASIA, Berg, 10MWT, 6MWT).
  • Run an early stroke rehabilitation pathway respecting AVERT 2015 evidence.
  • Identify candidates for constraint-induced movement therapy, intensive speech therapy, and dysphagia rehabilitation.
  • Run a spasticity ladder from triggers identification → physiotherapy → oral antispastics → botulinum toxin → intrathecal baclofen.
  • Use amantadine in TBI per Giacino NEJM 2012.
  • Apply LSVT BIG and LOUD in PD; cueing strategies for freezing of gait.
  • Manage SCI rehabilitation including bladder, bowel, sexual, pressure injury, autonomic dysreflexia.
  • Recognise when palliative neurorehabilitation principles apply.
The single principle: rehabilitation goals are set at the activity and participation levels, not at the impairment level. Restoring full strength may be impossible; restoring independent walking, return to work, and resuming family roles usually is. The patient and family define the goals; the team finds the route.

Lessons

1. Framework, ICF, recovery mechanisms
  • ICF (WHO 2001): body structure/function → activity → participation; influenced by environmental + personal factors.
  • Rehabilitation goals at activity + participation levels.
  • Recovery mechanisms: acute (oedema/reperfusion/diaschisis resolution) → subacute (unmasking + sprouting + map reorganisation + contralesional takeover) → chronic (use-dependent learning + compensation).

Kleim-Jones principles of experience-dependent plasticity: use it or lose it; use it and improve it; specificity; repetition; intensity; time; salience; age; transference; interference (learned non-use).

Adjuncts: dopaminergics for arousal (amantadine TBI Giacino NEJM 2012); SSRI for post-stroke depression (FLAME motor benefit not replicated FOCUS/EFFECTS/AFFINITY 2019-2020 but depression supported); rTMS / tDCS emerging; AVOID benzodiazepines + anticholinergics + typical antipsychotics (impair plasticity).

3. Stroke rehabilitation
  • AVERT 2015: very high-dose mobilisation within 24 h WORSE than usual care; early but short frequent sessions recommended; avoid very high doses in first 24 h.
  • Stroke unit care reduces mortality + dependency.
  • CIMT (EXCITE 2006): constrain unaffected limb 90% waking hours + intensive shaping affected limb × 2 weeks → upper limb function in patients with active wrist + finger extension.
  • Repetitive task-specific training; robotic upper limb (matched-dose equivalent); mental practice + mirror therapy; treadmill with BWS; FES for foot drop.
  • SSRI for motor recovery: FLAME 2011 positive but FOCUS / EFFECTS / AFFINITY 2019-2020 not replicated — not routinely used for motor recovery; still used for depression.
  • Aphasia: intensive SLT (high-dose > low-dose Brady Cochrane 2016); CIAT, melodic intonation, semantic feature analysis.
  • Dysphagia: bedside screen + VFSS/FEES + IDDSI diet + compensatory + rehabilitative + NG/PEG.
  • Neglect: prism adaptation + scanning + limb activation.
  • Shoulder subluxation + pain: support + FES + handling education.
  • Spasticity: see Section 11.
4. SCI rehabilitation
  • Acute (Modules 10, 22): stabilisation + pressure + VTE LMWH after 72 h + bowel/bladder + ASIA serially + autonomic dysreflexia prevention above T6.
  • Activity-based therapy: BWS treadmill + exoskeletons + epidural stimulation (Harkema, Courtine) emerging.
  • Wheelchair: prescription + skills + transfers.
  • Bladder: ISC > indwelling; suprapubic if needed; botulinum toxin detrusor; mirabegron; oxybutynin.
  • Bowel: scheduled programmes + digital stim + suppositories + gastrocolic reflex.
  • Sexual: PDE5; electroejaculation for fertility; pregnancy planning autonomic dysreflexia caveat.
  • Pressure injury prevention.
  • Spasticity: baclofen / tizanidine / gabapentin / BoNT / intrathecal baclofen.
  • Heterotopic ossification: bisphosphonate + NSAID + surgery when matured.
  • Long-term: CV risk + respiratory + chronic pain + depression + community + vocational + aging.
5. TBI rehabilitation
  • Disorders of consciousness: coma → VS (UWS) → MCS → emergence; CRS-R; amantadine 200–400 mg/day Giacino NEJM 2012 improves recovery in vegetative / MCS within 4 weeks.
  • PTA: time-orientation + memory impairment; duration predicts cognitive recovery (longer = worse).
  • Cognitive rehab: attention training (APT); memory (external aids, errorless learning, spaced retrieval); executive (goal management training); compensation strategies.
  • Behavioural / emotional: SSRI for mood; AVOID benzodiazepines + typical antipsychotics where possible. Post-traumatic seizures: LEV prophylaxis first week severe TBI.
  • Concussion / mTBI: most resolve 4 weeks; graded RTA/RTS (Berlin/Amsterdam 2022) — symptom-limited rest 24-48h then 6 stages 24h apart; relapse → previous stage; persistent post-concussion symptoms → MDT.
  • Second impact syndrome rare but catastrophic.
6. MS, PD, neuromuscular rehab
  • MS (Module 8): fatigue ~80% energy conservation + cooling + amantadine / modafinil; spasticity baclofen / BoNT / intrathecal; cognitive rehab; balance + AFO; bladder anticholinergic / ISC / BoNT; mood SSRI; multidisciplinary MS rehab.
  • PD (Module 6): LSVT BIG (amplitude movement) + LSVT LOUD (amplitude speech) — 16 sessions × 4 weeks; cueing strategies for freezing; treadmill; tai chi (Li NEJM 2012 balance); dance therapy; falls prevention; DBS programming.
  • HD rehab (Module 6).
  • GBS (Module 9): gradual physio as strength returns; cardiac monitoring acute autonomic; psych support; vocational.
  • MG (Module 9): energy conservation + activity scheduling around fatigability + medication timing + avoid contraindicated drugs.
  • ALS: multidisciplinary clinics improve survival + QoL; AAC + gastrostomy + NIV + palliative integration early.
  • Muscular dystrophy + SMA (Module 16 transformed by gene therapy) + chronic neuropathies.
7. Spasticity ladder

Modified Ashworth Scale (MAS) or Tardieu Scale.

  1. Identify + remove triggers: pain, infection (UTI), constipation, pressure injury, ill-fitting equipment, mood.
  2. Physiotherapy: stretching + positioning + splinting + strengthen antagonists + functional task training.
  3. Oral antispastics:
    • Baclofen: GABA-B; 5 mg TID → 80 mg/d; sedation; AVOID abrupt withdrawal (seizures, hyperthermia).
    • Tizanidine: α2 agonist; hypotension; hepatotoxicity (monitor LFTs).
    • Gabapentin / pregabalin (especially with neuropathic pain).
    • Dantrolene (peripheral; hepatotoxicity; MS, CP).
  4. Focal: botulinum toxin A 2-4 weeks onset + 3 months duration; phenol / alcohol nerve block.
  5. Intrathecal baclofen pump: severe diffuse spasticity (CP, MS, SCI); withdrawal life-threatening.
  6. Surgical: selective dorsal rhizotomy (paediatric CP); tendon lengthening / transfer; nerve transfer.
8. Technology + community reintegration
  • FES: foot drop; upper limb cycling; SCI.
  • Robot-assisted training: Lokomat / InMotion / Armeo — matched-dose equivalent to conventional.
  • Exoskeletons: ReWalk / Ekso / EksoNR for SCI walking; selected.
  • BCIs: BrainGate, Synchron, Neuralink — motor + communication; ALS / paralysis.
  • Non-invasive brain stimulation: rTMS for depression, aphasia, motor; tDCS — adjuncts.
  • VR: motor + neglect + exposure.
  • Telerehabilitation: video-delivered; expanded since COVID-19; cost-effective.
  • Wearables + apps: monitor + remote + gamification.
  • Epidural stimulation (Courtine) for chronic SCI emerging.

Community + vocational: RTW with reasonable adjustments + graded return; driving assessment + reporting per country; home assessment + modifications; respite + carer support; advance care planning; disability benefits.

Palliative neurorehabilitation: progressive incurable disease (ALS, end-stage MS, advanced PD, brain tumour, advanced dementia) — goals shift restoration → adaptation → maintenance → comfort.

Case Vignettes

Case 1 — A 65-year-old man 3 weeks after a right MCA infarct with NIHSS 14. He has dense left hemiparesis, mild left neglect, dysphagia (NG fed), and intact cognition. He is medically stable

Family motivated and supportive; lives in a single-story house. Previously independent + working part-time as an accountant.

Diagnosis: Moderate-severity stroke requiring inpatient rehabilitation.

Plan:

  1. Transfer to inpatient rehabilitation unit — stroke unit care reduces mortality + dependency; structured MDT.
  2. Goal-setting with patient + family using ICF framework: activity (independent transfers, walking 50 m with stick) + participation (return to work part-time within 6 months, drive within 12).
  3. Physiotherapy: high-dose task-specific training; gait training + treadmill with body-weight support if needed; AFO if foot drop emerges.
  4. Occupational therapy: upper limb retraining; ADLs; constraint-induced movement therapy if some active wrist + finger extension; environmental adaptation; cognitive rehab for neglect (prism adaptation + scanning training + limb activation).
  5. SLT: aphasia therapy if present; VFSS / FEES for dysphagia; IDDSI diet progression; PEG decision if no progress at 4 weeks.
  6. Spasticity: monitor; physiotherapy; baclofen / tizanidine if diffuse; botulinum toxin A if focal (equinovarus, clenched fist, hip adductors).
  7. Mood: screen for post-stroke depression; SSRI (sertraline) if present + CBT.
  8. Discharge planning: home assessment; carer education; community physiotherapy + OT + SLT; vocational rehabilitation; driving assessment at 6 months.
  9. Outcome measures: mRS, mBI, FIM, 10MWT serially.
  10. Cross-ref Modules 2, 17 (post-stroke pain), 24 (post-stroke depression).
Case 2 — A 30-year-old man 6 weeks after C5-AIS B (sensory incomplete tetraplegia) from a diving injury, transferred from ICU after neurosurgery + tracheostomy decannulation

Previously working in construction. Married with 2 young children.

Plan — SCI rehabilitation:

  1. Specialist SCI rehabilitation unit if available; high-intensity multidisciplinary care.
  2. Serial ASIA assessments; goal-setting at activity + participation levels.
  3. Activity-based therapy: high-dose task-specific motor training; consider body-weight-supported treadmill training + exoskeleton-assisted standing.
  4. Wheelchair prescription: powered (likely given C5 tetraplegia); cushioning; tilt-in-space.
  5. Bladder: intermittent self-catheterisation (with carer / partner assistance initially); avoid long-term indwelling; UTI surveillance.
  6. Bowel: scheduled programme; gastrocolic reflex; digital stim.
  7. Sexual function: counselling; PDE5; fertility specialist (electroejaculation).
  8. Pressure injury prevention: position changes every 2 h initially; pressure-relief cushions + mattress; skin inspection education.
  9. Autonomic dysreflexia education (T6 and above — this patient is at risk): patient + family education; sit-up + remove trigger + nifedipine IR or topical nitrate; medical alert card.
  10. Spasticity: baclofen first-line; intrathecal baclofen pump if severe + refractory.
  11. Respiratory: secretion management; pulmonary care; flu / pneumococcal vaccination.
  12. Mood + psychosocial: clinical psychology; peer support; carer education; long-term mental health support.
  13. Vocational rehabilitation: education / re-skilling; possible community employment options; financial support.
  14. Long-term: cardiovascular surveillance; bone health (osteoporosis from immobilisation); aging with SCI clinic surveillance.
  15. Cross-ref Modules 10, 15, 22.
Case 3 — A 25-year-old man 6 weeks after severe TBI with diffuse axonal injury. GCS 9 on admission. Currently in minimally conscious state (CRS-R 11). Family considering withdrawal

Spontaneous eye opening; visual tracking + occasional command-following present. No verbal output. Tube-fed via PEG.

Diagnosis: Minimally conscious state after severe TBI; within window for amantadine.

Plan:

  1. Avoid premature withdrawal — recovery from MCS can occur over months; multidisciplinary discussion + family meetings + advance directive review.
  2. Amantadine 100 mg BID titrated to 200 mg BID (Giacino NEJM 2012) — improves recovery in vegetative / minimally conscious states when started within 4 weeks of injury (acceptable to start at 6 weeks); continue until plateau, then trial off.
  3. Other agents (modafinil, methylphenidate) emerging — consult.
  4. Serial CRS-R assessment — track emergence to consciousness.
  5. AVOID benzodiazepines, anticholinergics, and typical antipsychotics — impair plasticity and consciousness recovery.
  6. Manage post-traumatic seizures (LEV preferred); manage spasticity; tracheostomy + PEG care.
  7. When emerging, structured cognitive rehabilitation (attention + memory + executive function).
  8. Family-centred care; psychological support; vocational rehabilitation planning over months.
  9. If no recovery emerges, transition to long-term care with palliative principles.
  10. Cross-ref Modules 22, 24.
Case 4 — A 22-year-old footballer concussed in a tackle; loss of consciousness for 30 seconds; headache + dizziness + difficulty concentrating now

No focal neurology. CT brain normal.

Diagnosis: Concussion / mild TBI.

Plan — Berlin / Amsterdam (2022) Consensus Concussion in Sport Group framework:

  1. Symptom-limited cognitive + physical rest 24–48 hours; observe for red flags (deterioration, focal signs, seizures).
  2. Graded return-to-sport / return-to-activity through 6 stages with 24 h between stages; relapse of symptoms → return to previous stage:
    1. Symptom-limited daily activity.
    2. Light aerobic exercise (walking, stationary cycle).
    3. Sport-specific exercise (no head impact).
    4. Non-contact training.
    5. Full-contact practice (medical clearance required).
    6. Return to play.
  3. Address specific symptoms: headache (paracetamol short-term; avoid medication overuse — Module 4); sleep (sleep hygiene); mood; vestibular (physiotherapy); cervical (physiotherapy).
  4. Persistent post-concussion symptoms (PPCS, > 4 weeks): multidisciplinary management; CBT for anxiety / depression; vestibular physiotherapy; gradual return to activity; education + reassurance.
  5. Counsel about second impact syndrome — diffuse cerebral oedema risk if second injury before first has resolved (rare but catastrophic).
  6. Repeated concussion + CTE risk discussion for retirement / equipment changes (boxing, American football).
Case 5 — A 70-year-old with moderate Parkinson disease (Hoehn-Yahr III) has progressive hypophonia (soft voice) and freezing of gait with multiple near-falls

On levodopa-carbidopa + ropinirole. Cognition intact.

Plan — multidisciplinary PD rehabilitation:

  1. LSVT LOUD for hypophonia: 16 sessions over 4 weeks; amplitude-focused speech therapy; improves loudness + intelligibility; refer to a certified LSVT LOUD clinician.
  2. LSVT BIG for motor symptoms: 16 sessions over 4 weeks; amplitude-focused movement therapy; improves bradykinesia + posture.
  3. Cueing strategies for freezing of gait: visual (lines on floor, target with foot), auditory (metronome, music with beat), tactile (vibrating cue); STARTSTOP strategy at doorways.
  4. Treadmill training for gait speed (if safe).
  5. Falls prevention: home assessment; remove tripping hazards; install grab rails; appropriate footwear; tai chi (Li NEJM 2012 — improves balance in PD); referral to multidisciplinary falls clinic.
  6. Review medications: optimise dopaminergic timing (especially around speech / mobility tasks); consider DBS referral if motor fluctuations not controlled (Module 6).
  7. Carer education; community PD support group; consider community-based exercise programmes (Rock Steady Boxing, dance therapy — patient choice).
  8. Cross-ref Module 6.
Case 6 — A 55-year-old with ALS has progressive bulbar dysfunction (dysarthria + dysphagia) and respiratory decline (FVC 60%). He is considering NIV and feeding options

He has 2 young adult children. Lives with wife. Previously working as a teacher.

Plan — multidisciplinary ALS rehabilitation:

  1. Multidisciplinary ALS clinic — improves survival + quality of life vs general clinic.
  2. Respiratory: pulmonary function tests + sniff nasal inspiratory pressure (SNIP); discuss NIV at FVC < 50% predicted or symptomatic respiratory failure (improves survival + QoL); cough assist devices; secretion management.
  3. Bulbar: SLT for dysarthria + dysphagia; augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) — picture boards, speech-generating devices, eye-gaze technology, BCIs in selected patients; voice banking early before voice deteriorates.
  4. Nutrition: dietitian; gastrostomy (PEG / RIG) when oral intake compromised + weight loss (better placed earlier when FVC > 50% to reduce procedural risk).
  5. Mobility: physiotherapy + OT + AFO + walking aids + wheelchair (powered when needed).
  6. Spasticity / cramps: baclofen, gabapentin; quinine for cramps.
  7. Sialorrhoea: glycopyrrolate, atropine drops, botulinum toxin to salivary glands.
  8. Pseudobulbar affect: dextromethorphan-quinidine.
  9. Riluzole + (where available) edaravone; AMX0035 withdrawn 2024; tofersen for SOD1-ALS (cross-ref Module 9).
  10. Psychological + family + palliative care integration early: ACP (advance care planning); hospice; bereavement support.
  11. End-of-life: discuss preferences for NIV, tracheostomy, gastrostomy, place of care, comfort measures; respect autonomy.
  12. Cross-ref Modules 9 (ALS), 14 (sleep / respiratory), 17 (palliative pain).
